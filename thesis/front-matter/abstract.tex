\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Compositional generalization failure and AI alignment failure are the same phenomenon}
— a bifurcation in the agent's internal \emph{schema coherence} (the $\sigma$-trap) — and solving one
solves the other. This monograph develops and defends that claim in three movements.

\textbf{Diagnosis (Ch.~2--3).} A scoping review of the AGI safety landscape and a systematic review of the
$\sigma$-trap evidence base show that the safety literature is fragmented and that no existing framework
treats internal schema coherence as the central failure mode; the reviews map the terrain without
presupposing the thesis.

\textbf{Framework and evidence (Ch.~4--8).} The $\Sigma$-Align framework formalizes schema coherence as
a measurable, intervenable quantity. A pilot study tests $\sigma$-coupling interventions, a meta-analysis
quantifies the $\sigma$-trap across the literature, and two empirical studies show that compositional
generalization failure is a real bifurcation in a minimal mechanistic model (the $\Sigma$-Model) and that
the trap is safety-relevant through mesa-optimization.

\textbf{Implications (Ch.~9--10).} Schema-coherent training is argued to be the optimal path to safe,
long-term AGI, with direct relevance to Corrigibility, CEV, and Indirect Normativity.

\textbf{Keywords:} compositional generalisation, schema coherence, AI alignment, AGI safety, neural ODEs,
dynamical systems, mesa-optimization, curriculum learning.
\end{abstract}
