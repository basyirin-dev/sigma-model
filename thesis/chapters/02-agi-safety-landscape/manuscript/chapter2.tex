\section{Why map the safety landscape}

AGI safety has grown from a predominantly theoretical concern into a rapidly expanding
field spanning alignment, robustness, interpretability, evaluation, governance, and
emergent failure modes \citep{hendrycks2023overview,hendrycks2021unsolved,ji2023survey}.
The expansion has produced substantial progress, but also a fragmented evidence base in
which related problems are studied under different concepts, methods, and standards of
evidence. In particular, research on alignment and research on generalisation have
developed as largely distinct literatures, despite their shared concern with whether
learned systems behave appropriately when conditions differ from those encountered
during training \citep{amodei2016concrete,lake2018generalization,keysers2020measuring,
geirhos2020shortcut,hupkes2023taxonomy}.

Existing reviews offer partial maps. Reviews of alignment, safety, and risk have
differed in scope, terminology, publication coverage, and methodological emphasis
\citep{triantafyllopoulos2026value,shen2024bidirectional,gyevnar2025aisafety,
mclean2021risks,slattery2024ai,ahmed2024future}. None of the closest prior reviews
integrates the three elements mapped here together: the field's subdomains, its methods
and formalisms, and its treatment of internal representation. The closest predecessors
cover value alignment only (Triantafyllopoulos et al., 83 sources), alignment broadly
(Shen et al., 400+), AI safety broadly (Gyevnar \& Kasirzadeh, 383), AGI risk (McLean
et al., 16), or risk domains (Slattery et al., 777 risks) --- but none maps all three
elements simultaneously, and none is registered and protocol-driven in the sense used
here.

The gap is practical and theoretical. Practically, researchers lack a single account of
where the safety literature is concentrated, which areas are supported by stronger or
weaker evidence, and which methodological approaches remain underdeveloped.
Theoretically, it remains unclear how the compositional generalisation literature
relates to the alignment literature, and whether internal representation structure is
treated as a central safety property or merely as an explanatory mechanism.

Two boundary decisions shape the map. First, AGI safety is distinct from narrow AI
safety: narrow safety addresses the immediate harms of deployed, task-specific systems
(bias, privacy, adversarial vulnerability), whereas AGI safety addresses the structural
and existential hazards of highly generalised, autonomous, self-improving systems
--- inner misalignment, corrigibility failure, loss of control
\citep{hendrycks2023overview,russell2019human,bostrom2014superintelligence}. A system
that must remain aligned across an unbounded distribution of novel tasks poses
questions that do not arise for a single-purpose classifier \citep{russell2019human}.
Second, the review covers English-language research published 2015--2026 across
peer-reviewed and grey-literature sources, with searches executed July--August 2026.

\section{Three waves and an unsettled vocabulary}

The vocabulary of AGI safety is not canonical, and that absence is itself a finding.
Core terms --- alignment, safety, robustness, interpretability --- carry different
meanings across the decision-theoretic tradition that founded the field, the
machine-learning community that now dominates it empirically, and the governance
literature that regulates it \citep{bostrom2014superintelligence,russell2019human,
hendrycks2023overview}. Value alignment, for example, denotes both a technical target
--- an objective function consistent with human preferences \citep{amodei2016concrete}
--- and a contested normative project \citep{triantafyllopoulos2026value}. Rather than
imposing a taxonomy, the review charts the taxonomy the literature actually exhibits,
defining each term operationally where it is used (SQ1).

The field has developed in identifiable waves, each carrying framing assumptions that
the next wave inherits or rejects. Early work in the 2000s was theoretical: it
formalised goal structures such as Coherent Extrapolated Volition \citep{yudkowsky2004cev}
and the control problem, treating the agent as an abstract optimiser whose specification
could be reasoned about in isolation \citep{bostrom2014superintelligence,
soares2015corrigibility}. The deep-learning era of the 2010s reframed safety around
concrete, empirical failure modes --- reward hacking, safe exploration, distributional
shift --- and around the \emph{behaviour} of trained systems rather than their internal
structure \citep{amodei2016concrete,hendrycks2021unsolved,hubinger2019risks}. That
behavioural framing proved enormously productive, but rests on an assumption the third
wave, from roughly 2021 onward, explicitly challenges: that a model's internal states
are either irrelevant to safety or unobservable. The internal-structure wave ---
eliciting latent knowledge \citep{christiano2021elk}, natural abstractions
\citep{wentworth2020natural}, mechanistic interpretability \citep{bereska2024mechanistic},
the study of deceptive alignment \citep{hubinger2024sleeper} --- treats the model's
representations as the safety surface itself \citep{huvard2023notion}. This progression
from external behaviour to internal structure is the historical context in which schema
coherence and the $\sigma$-trap arise as organising concepts (SQ3).

\section{Methods: a registered scoping review}

A scoping review is the appropriate design for a mapping question about a broad,
emerging field with heterogeneous evidence, including grey literature
\citep{arksey2005scoping,levac2010scoping,tricco2018prismascr}. A systematic review
would be premature: the field lacks a settled outcome measure --- the same phenomenon
is reported as accuracy gaps, benchmark failures, and governance analyses --- and a
narrative review would not provide the reproducible, protocol-driven map a research
programme needs as its evidence base. The protocol was registered prospectively at
OSF (ntuh2), and reporting follows the PRISMA extension for Scoping Reviews
(PRISMA-ScR) \citep{tricco2018prismascr} (the completed 22-item checklist is carried
over from the source paper; the PRISMA flow is in Figure~\ref{fig:ch2-prisma}).

The question is decomposed into three component questions. RQ1 (mapping): how is the
AGI safety landscape structured across safety subdomains, methodological and formal
approaches, and the treatment of internal representation? RQ2 (intersection): where do
compositional generalisation and alignment research intersect? RQ3 (gaps): what
evidence gaps are relevant to schema-coherence accounts of AGI safety? Five registered
sub-questions operationalise these, using a PCC framework (population: the AGI/ML
safety research community; concept: safety frameworks, formal methods, failure modes,
and treatment of internal representation structure; context: peer-reviewed and grey
literature 2015--March 2026).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria (I1--I5, E1--E6) are defined in the protocol. Dual
screening with AI assistance was followed by full-text retrieval, charting, and a
quality assessment of all included studies. Credibility was scored on eight weighted
dimensions (D1--D8) combining source-based signals (venue tier, author authority,
citation uptake) with methodological signals (formal-methods rigour, empirical
reproducibility, argumentative rigour, transparency, prior-literature engagement),
using a paper-type-adjusted rubric adapted from QUADAS-2, ROBINS-I, and PROBAST+AI
\citep{whiting2011quadas2,sterne2016robinsi,moons2025probastaialpha}. A 20\% independent
consistency sample yielded per-dimension kappa 0.924--1.000 and a composite ICC of 0.947;
credibility is a signal, not a filter, and a sensitivity analysis compared conclusions
on the full set against the high-credibility subset.

\section{Results: five stories about the field}
\label{sec:ch2-results}

\subsection{What the field is}

The review included \textbf{1,136 unique studies} (from 1,268 records; 132 duplicate
versions --- typically an arXiv preprint and its published counterpart --- collapsed to
one entry). The corpus grew sharply: annual counts rose from 1 in 2015 to 344 in 2025
(275 in the partial year 2026), with 72.6\% of studies published in 2024--2026.
Empirical studies were most common (459; 40.4\%), followed by other (354; 31.2\%),
position papers (121; 10.7\%), theoretical studies (99; 8.7\%), reviews (80; 7.0\%),
and opinion pieces (23; 2.0\%). Venue coverage was fragmented: arXiv was the single
largest venue (176; 15.5\%), with the remainder spread across 520 raw venue strings
and a long tail accounting for half the corpus.

\subsection{What methods it uses}

Methodologically, 63.1\% of studies had no applicable empirical methodology recorded;
experiments were reported in 28.2\%, simulations in 4.6\%, analyses in 2.6\%, theory in
1.1\%, and case studies in 0.4\%. Under a permissive definition, 63.2\% of studies were
formal; 37.7\% named a formal framework (information theory 77, decision theory 64,
game theory 50, dynamical systems 18) and 57.2\% named a mathematical formalism
(optimization 486, probability 393, logic 207, ODEs 11). The share of formal content
grew over time. The methods gradient matters for the thesis: formal and mathematical
machinery is present in the field, but it is concentrated in specific subdomains and
is rarely brought to bear on representation structure.

\subsection{How credible its evidence is}

Composite credibility scores (median 2.15, IQR 1.90--2.35) placed 2 studies in tier A,
279 in tier B, 735 in tier C, 118 in tier D, and 2 in tier E --- a high-credibility
subset (A+B) of 281 studies (24.7\%). 120 studies (10.6\%) were flagged low-credibility.
Credibility varies systematically: capability-and-control work carries the highest
credibility among the large subdomains, while the evaluation-and-measurement theme ---
the second-largest theme in the corpus (251 studies) --- is simultaneously the least
formal and the least credible (17.9\% tier A+B).

\subsection{Whether the field studies internal structure}
\label{sec:ch2-structure}

Subdomain coverage is highly skewed (multi-select): value alignment 1,020 (89.8\%),
ethics 603 (53.1\%), robustness 537 (47.3\%), capabilities and control 406 (35.7\%),
interpretability 360 (31.7\%), governance 291 (25.6\%), mesa-optimization 73 (6.4\%),
other 38 (3.3\%). Thematic analysis grouped open codes into 13 axial themes in four
clusters. The \emph{reward-hacking cluster} links proxy-reward work (178 studies),
preference and value learning (123), and robustness (140) --- the strongest couplings
in the corpus, with proxy misspecification the fastest-growing theme (from 3 studies in
2023 to 70 in 2025). The \emph{schema-coherence cluster} links internal representation
structure and schema (88 studies) with compositional generalization and the
$\sigma$-trap (158; coupling 0.183): representation structure is discussed
operationally --- features, activations, latents --- rather than axiomatically. Only
176 studies (15.5\%) discuss internal representations at all, and only 14 use the
phrase ``internal representation'' literally. This is the single most important
finding for this monograph: \emph{the field rarely treats internal representation
structure as a safety property}.

\subsection{Where the bridge is}

The compositional-generalisation--alignment intersection is thin. The corpus contains
substantial literatures on each side, but the bridging studies are few, and the
schema-coherence signal --- a heuristic mapping variable defined by the review, not
evidence that included studies endorse the $\sigma$-trap construct --- is present in
40.8\% of the corpus, rising to 49.5\% in the high-credibility subset. The identified
gaps (G1--G5) include the absence of a formal measure of representation coherence, the
absence of a mechanistic account linking compositional failure to alignment failure,
and the thin coverage of mesa-optimization (73 studies; 6.4\%) relative to its role in
alignment-failure accounts --- 41.4\% of that work is high-credibility (e.g., deceptive
alignment and monitoring proposals), but it remains thinly covered. One further absence
deserves explicit note: \emph{goal misgeneralization} --- the alignment community's term
for a system that generalises its proxy goal into novel settings
\citep{langosco2022goal} --- did not appear as a charted concept in the records, even
though it is a direct candidate expression of the very mechanism this monograph studies.
Chapter~3's systematic review and Chapter~8 return to this concept to ask whether it is
another name for a low-$\sigma$ regime and why the keyword mapping missed it.

\section{Discussion}

The landscape has a coherent shape: a large, fast-growing, formally uneven field in
which value alignment is the ubiquitous framing, robustness and interpretability are
the most empirical large subdomains, governance is the least empirical but
highest-credibility, and measurement --- the very thing a schema-coherence account
needs --- is the weakest area of all. The credibility distribution is a property of the
corpus, not of the scoring process (the consistency statistics make that clear), and
the headline conclusions are robust to restricting to high-credibility studies.

The most consequential finding for the thesis is the structure gap. The field's
history shows a progression from external behaviour to internal structure, yet the
internal-representation literature remains operational and fragmented: 15.5\% of
studies touch internal representations, and almost none treat them axiomatically or
measure them. If the $\sigma$-trap thesis is correct, this is the gap where the
evidence for it should appear --- and where its absence is currently most acute. The
review itself is neutral: it establishes the baseline, not the claim. The discipline
is worth stating plainly: a literature gap establishes \emph{novelty}, not
\emph{truth}; the absence of schema coherence in the literature is evidence that the
field has not yet measured it, not evidence about what such a measure would find. The
subsequent chapters must earn every tier of the claim on their own evidence, exactly
as Chapter~1's falsifiability criteria demand. The review's limitation is inherent to
scoping designs --- it charts rather than synthesises, and the heuristic
schema-coherence variable is a mapping device, not a validated construct.

\section{Relation to Other Chapters}

narrows from the landscape to the $\sigma$-trap evidence base, relying on the
subdomain and theme structure charted here (particularly the schema-coherence cluster
