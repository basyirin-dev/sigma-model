\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background:} AGI safety has grown into a large, fast-moving field, but existing reviews are fragmented across subdomains and under-cover the preprint and forum literature where much of the field develops. Whether the compositional-generalisation and alignment literatures are connected remains open.

\textbf{Objectives:} To map the English-language AGI safety literature (2015--2026, peer-reviewed and grey) into a subdomain taxonomy, a catalogue of methods, an account of how internal representation structure is treated, and a gap analysis.

\textbf{Methods:} We conducted a PRISMA-ScR scoping review across six databases plus grey literature. Of 5,191 records identified, 2,867 were screened and 1,268 studies were included. Structured fields were charted with a hybrid extraction pipeline (validation ICC 0.930); credibility was scored on an eight-dimension rubric (validation kappa 0.924--1.000); 13 axial themes were derived by open coding.

\textbf{Results:} The corpus grew sharply (71.8\% from 2024--2026). Value alignment dominated (90.1\%); mesa-optimization was thin (6.5\%). Only 25.0\% of studies reached the high-credibility tiers; evaluation work was large (281 studies) but low-credibility (17.8\%). Internal representations were discussed by 16.0\% of studies, but ``compositional generalization'' appeared in 48, ``goal misgeneralization'' in none, and sigma-trap-relevant studies (46.3\%) rarely intersected compositional-generalisation work (8 studies).

\textbf{Conclusions:} The AGI safety landscape is mapped: the ingredients of a schema-coherence account are present, the connecting joints are missing, and the evidence base is uneven. These findings bound and motivate the thesis papers that follow.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
% ============================================================

Artificial general intelligence (AGI) safety has moved from a niche philosophical concern to a central problem of computer science and technical governance. Frontier systems now exhibit broad, domain-transcending capabilities, and the hazards associated with those capabilities---misaligned objectives, deceptive behaviour, and out-of-distribution (OOD) failure---are no longer hypothetical~\cite{amodei2016concrete,bostrom2014superintelligence,russell2019human}. The field's founding analyses framed the core difficulty as a control problem: how to build systems that pursue objectives reliably aligned with human values even as their capabilities exceed human-level performance on an expanding range of tasks~\cite{bostrom2014superintelligence}. Two decades of subsequent work have produced a large and rapidly growing body of research on this problem, spanning value alignment, interpretability, robustness, governance, and the study of emergent failure modes~\cite{hendrycks2023overview,hendrycks2021unsolved,ji2023survey}.

AGI safety is distinct from narrow AI safety. Narrow AI safety addresses the immediate harms of deployed, task-specific systems---algorithmic bias, privacy violations, and adversarial vulnerability. AGI safety addresses the structural and existential hazards of highly generalised, autonomous, and self-improving systems, including inner misalignment, corrigibility failure, and loss of control~\cite{hendrycks2023overview,russell2019human}. The distinction matters because the failure modes, the formal tools, and the governance regimes differ: a system that must remain aligned across an unbounded distribution of novel tasks poses questions that do not arise for a single-purpose classifier~\cite{russell2019human}.

The empirical literature that now constitutes the bulk of AGI safety research is organised around a shared observation: models trained to optimise in-distribution performance frequently fail---sometimes catastrophically---when deployed out of distribution. Shortcut learning, compositional generalisation failure, and domain shift have each been studied extensively and largely independently~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut,lake2018generalization,lake2023compositional,hupkes2023taxonomy,keysers2020measuring,liu2021towards,zhou2023domain,wang2022generalizing,karbasian2025unified,matta2024systematic}. A parallel literature studies misalignment as a failure of the same kind: a model optimising a proxy objective, or a goal that generalises poorly beyond its training environment~\cite{amodei2016concrete}. Whether these two families of failure---compositional generalisation failure and alignment failure---are connected, and if so how, remains open.

Existing reviews of this landscape are real but fragmented. Recent systematic and scoping reviews have covered value alignment in advisory AI~\cite{triantafyllopoulos2026value}, bidirectional human-AI alignment~\cite{shen2024bidirectional}, and broad AI safety with an explicit focus on peer-reviewed sources~\cite{gyevnar2025aisafety}; a meta-review of risk taxonomies has catalogued over 700 risks across seven domains, and earlier reviews of AGI risk predate the current empirical wave~\cite{hendrycks2023overview,mclean2021risks,slattery2024ai,ahmed2024future}. Their coverage is uneven in three ways that matter for a research programme organised around internal representation structure. First, preprint and forum literature---where much of the field actually develops---is under-represented, so the reviews systematically miss the grey-literature half of the corpus. Second, terminology is unsettled: alignment, safety, and robustness are used with different meanings across the decision-theoretic, machine-learning, and governance literatures, which limits comparability across studies and reviews alike~\cite{gyevnar2025aisafety,shen2024bidirectional,triantafyllopoulos2026value}. Third, no review integrates the safety subdomains, the formal methods used across them, and the treatment of internal representation structure into a single map. The consequence of this fragmentation is practical: a researcher asking ``where is the field strong, where is it thin, and where does the treatment of model structure sit within it?'' cannot currently be answered from a single source, and the field's growth---the corpus more than doubled in the final two years of this review's window---makes the gap harder to close with time.

A scoping review is the appropriate design for this question, and the choice is deliberate. The question is a mapping question: it asks how a broad, emerging field is structured, which methods are used where, and where the gaps are, rather than asking whether a specific intervention works against a comparator. Scoping reviews are the established methodology for exactly this situation---broad or emerging topics with heterogeneous evidence, including grey literature, where the goal is to chart concepts and gaps rather than to synthesise a homogeneous outcome~\cite{arksey2005scoping,levac2010scoping,tricco2018prismascr}. A systematic review would be premature here because the field lacks a settled outcome measure (the same phenomenon is reported as accuracy gaps, benchmark failures, and governance analyses), and a narrative review would not provide the reproducible, protocol-driven map that a research programme needs as its evidence base. The scoping design therefore follows from the research question rather than being imposed on it.

This review is one component of a thesis-by-publication project organised around a specific thesis: that compositional generalisation failure and alignment failure are manifestations of the same underlying phenomenon, the ``$\sigma$-trap''---a low-schema-coherence equilibrium in which a model internalises surface statistics rather than the generative structure of its training distribution~\cite{amsyar2026sigma}. Schema coherence ($\sigma_A$) refers to the degree to which a model's internal representations reflect the latent generative structure of the data~\cite{huvard2023notion}. The present review does not attempt to establish this thesis; its contribution is deliberately narrower and incremental: to map the AGI safety landscape as it currently exists, to quantify how the literature distributes across subdomains, methodologies, and credibility levels, and to document the gaps---including the treatment of internal representation structure---that a schema-coherence framework would need to address~\cite{amsyar2026sigma}.

The research question guiding this scoping review, specified prospectively in a registered protocol (https://osf.io/ntuh2/), is: \emph{How is the landscape of AGI safety research structured in terms of subdomains, formal methods, and the treatment of internal representation structure, and what gaps exist that a schema-coherence framework could address?} The review is organised around five sub-questions: (SQ1) what taxonomy of subdomains does the literature exhibit; (SQ2) how are formal methods and mathematical formalisms distributed across the field; (SQ3) how is internal representation structure treated in safety research; (SQ4) how do the compositional generalisation and alignment literatures relate; and (SQ5) what gaps exist that a schema-coherence framework could address. Five objectives map onto these sub-questions: to produce a subdomain taxonomy, a catalogue of methods, a structure-safety map, a gap analysis, and a thesis-aligned evidence map.

The remainder of this paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:background} defines the core concepts and traces the development of the field. Section~\ref{sec:methods} describes the scoping-review methodology, following the PRISMA extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR)~\cite{tricco2018prismascr} (see Appendix~\ref{app:prisma-scr} for the completed 22-item checklist). Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the results: a descriptive numerical summary, subdomain-by-subdomain findings, cross-cutting themes, and an analysis of gaps. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} interprets the findings, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Background}\label{sec:background}
% ============================================================

The vocabulary of AGI safety is unsettled, and the absence of a canonical taxonomy is itself a finding about the field. The core terms---alignment, safety, robustness, interpretability---are used with different meanings across the decision-theoretic tradition that founded the field, the machine-learning community that now dominates it empirically, and the governance literature that regulates it~\cite{bostrom2014superintelligence,russell2019human,hendrycks2023overview}. Value alignment, for example, denotes both a technical target (an objective function consistent with human preferences~\cite{amodei2016concrete}) and a contested normative project (whose values, elicited how, and aggregated under which conditions~\cite{triantafyllopoulos2026value}). This terminological pluralism is a genuine weakness: it limits comparability across studies, complicates synthesis, and lets superficially similar claims talk past one another~\cite{gyevnar2025aisafety,shen2024bidirectional}. It is also, however, the honest state of a field whose boundaries are still being drawn, and it motivates the first sub-question of this review (SQ1): rather than imposing a taxonomy, we chart the taxonomy the literature actually exhibits, and we define each term operationally where we use it.

The field has developed in identifiable waves, each with a distinct object of study, and each wave's framing carries assumptions that the next wave inherits or rejects. Early work in the 2000s was theoretical: it formalised goal structures such as Coherent Extrapolated Volition~\cite{yudkowsky2004cev} and the control problem, treating the agent as an abstract optimiser whose specification could be reasoned about in isolation~\cite{bostrom2014superintelligence,soares2015corrigibility}. The deep-learning era of the 2010s reframed safety around concrete, empirical failure modes---reward hacking, safe exploration, distributional shift---and, crucially, around the behaviour of trained systems rather than their internal structure~\cite{amodei2016concrete,hendrycks2021unsolved,hubinger2019risks}. This behavioural framing proved enormously productive, but it rests on an assumption that the third wave, from roughly 2021 onward, explicitly challenges: that the internal states of a model are either irrelevant to safety or unobservable. The internal-structure wave---eliciting latent knowledge~\cite{christiano2021elk}, natural abstractions~\cite{wentworth2020natural}, mechanistic interpretability~\cite{bereska2024mechanistic}, the study of deceptive alignment~\cite{hubinger2024sleeper}---treats the model's representations as the safety surface itself~\cite{huvard2023notion}. This progression from external behaviour to internal structure is the historical context in which schema coherence and the $\sigma$-trap arise as organising concepts, and it motivates SQ3: how, and how rigorously, does the current literature treat internal representation structure as a safety property~\cite{amsyar2026sigma}?

Schema coherence ($\sigma_A$) is the degree to which a model's internal representations reflect the latent generative structure of the data. The $\sigma$-trap is the failure regime in which training dynamics suppress schema coherence, producing models that fit the surface statistics of their training distribution and therefore fail to generalise compositionally or remain aligned under distribution shift~\cite{amsyar2026sigma}. The thesis underlying this project treats compositional generalisation failure and alignment failure as two faces of this single phenomenon. Two caveats govern the use of this lens here, and both follow from the critical stance taken throughout this review. First, the lens is novel: schema coherence has no established measurement instrument, and its closest relatives in the literature---goal misgeneralisation~\cite{langosco2022goal}, mesa-optimisation~\cite{hubinger2019risks}, and mechanistic-interpretability constructs such as features and circuits~\cite{bereska2024mechanistic}---are operationalised differently in different papers~\cite{amodei2016concrete,huvard2023notion}. Second, the review therefore uses the lens heuristically, as an organising device for asking where the ingredients of the thesis are present in the corpus and where the connecting joints are missing, without claiming that the thesis is established by the review itself. These caveats motivate SQ4 and SQ5: whether the compositional-generalisation and alignment literatures relate at all, and which gaps a schema-coherence framework would need to close~\cite{amsyar2026sigma,huvard2023notion}.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}
% ============================================================

This scoping review was conducted and reported in accordance with the PRISMA extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR)~\cite{tricco2018prismascr,arksey2005scoping,levac2010scoping}, with a protocol registered prospectively on the Open Science Framework (https://osf.io/ntuh2/).

\subsection{Protocol and Registration}
The protocol---including the research question and sub-questions, objectives, eligibility criteria, search strategy, data-charting plan, quality-assessment plan, and synthesis plan---was specified in full before the search began and registered prospectively on the Open Science Framework (https://osf.io/ntuh2/), following the reporting requirements for scoping-review protocols~\cite{arksey2005scoping,levac2010scoping,shamseer2015prismap,tricco2018prismascr}. The review is reported against the 22-item PRISMA-ScR checklist~\cite{tricco2018prismascr} (Appendix~\ref{app:prisma-scr}); the completed checklist and the PRISMA flow diagram (Figure~\ref{fig:prisma-flow}) follow the corresponding reporting guidance~\cite{page2021prisma2020}. No protocol amendments were required.

\subsection{Eligibility Criteria}
Studies were eligible if they addressed AGI safety as defined in Section~\ref{sec:background}---that is, the structural, technical, or existential hazards of highly generalised systems---rather than narrow AI safety alone. The Population-Concept-Context (PCC) framework, the scoping-review analogue of PICO~\cite{arksey2005scoping,levac2010scoping}, was used to structure the criteria: the population is the AI/AGI research community; the concept is AGI safety frameworks, approaches, and failure modes; the context is peer-reviewed and grey literature published between 2015 and 2026. Inclusion required (i) a structural AGI-safety component, (ii) publication in 2015--2026, and (iii) English language. Records addressing only narrow AI safety concerns (algorithmic bias, privacy, post-hoc interpretability of narrow systems) without a structural AGI-safety component were excluded, as were records outside the date range or not in English.

Two restrictions merit explicit justification. The 2015 lower bound coincides with the empirical reframing of the field: the deep-learning-era formulation of concrete safety problems, which defines the modern research programme, was published in 2016~\cite{amodei2016concrete}, and the pre-2015 literature is largely theoretical and already covered by prior reviews of AGI risk~\cite{bostrom2014superintelligence,hendrycks2023overview}. Restricting to English follows the precedent of comparable reviews of this field and reflects practical access constraints; the grey-literature sources included (arXiv, PhilPapers, the AI Alignment Forum) are predominantly English-language~\cite{gyevnar2025aisafety}.

\subsection{Information Sources and Search Strategy}
The search was built as a block-structured intersection query around three concept blocks---safety, generalisation, and internal structure---because pilot searches confirmed that flat union queries return tens of thousands of records while the intersection of safety and structural-generalisation terms is where the thesis-relevant literature actually lives~\cite{hendrycks2023overview,sinha2024systematic}. Four query families were defined (F1 broad safety; F2 safety $\cap$ generalisation; F3 narrow safety $\cap$ structure $\cap$ safety qualifiers; F4 schema-coherence exploratory) and adapted to the syntax of each database. We searched six databases: Scopus, Web of Science, ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, arXiv, and PhilPapers, executed in July--August 2026 with coverage 2015--2026; the full database-specific strings are in Appendix~\ref{app:search-strings}. The database search was supplemented with grey literature and citation tracking: Google Scholar for grey sources, OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar for forward/backward citation chaining from the protocol's seed papers, and manual inclusion of the AI Alignment Forum corpus. Venue and terminology planning drew on the phase-0.5 venue analysis, which documented the field's ~50\% preprint rate and its dependence on workshop and grey sources~\cite{gyevnar2025aisafety}.

\subsection{Screening}
Records were deduplicated programmatically (exact-title and DOI matching), removing 2,324 duplicate records from the 5,191 identified. Screening proceeded in three stages: title screening, abstract screening, and full-text assessment, performed by the single author using Rayyan~\cite{ouzzani2016rayyan} with a calibration set used to fix inclusion thresholds before the main pass. Figure~\ref{fig:prisma-flow} summarises the flow of records. A total of 5,191 records were identified from databases and supplementary sources; after removing 2,324 duplicates, 2,867 records were screened at title and abstract level, and 1,589 were excluded. The remaining 1,278 records proceeded to full-text assessment, of which 10 were excluded (3 for not addressing AGI-safety structure, 7 for no AGI-safety subdomain engagement), leaving 1,268 included studies. Exclusion reasons at screening were recorded in four categories---not structural AGI safety, no AGI-safety subdomain engagement, outside the 2015--2026 date range, and not in English---mirroring the eligibility criteria.

\subsection{Data Extraction}
A 25-field charting schema was developed, piloted on five studies spanning the subdomain and publication-type space, and finalised before the full run (available in the supplementary materials). The fields fall into six groups: bibliographic (paper identifier, title, authors, year, venue, DOI, citation count); classification (publication type, subdomains, methodology); formal content (formal framework, mathematical formalism, key equations and definitions); structure-relevance (whether the study discusses internal representations, whether it discusses schema coherence, its sigma-trap relevance rating, and the justification for that rating); empirical detail (datasets used, sample size, effect sizes); and reporting (key contribution, limitations stated, open questions, evidence basis, notes). Structured fields were charted by a hybrid pipeline: scripted heuristic extraction from full texts, followed by a large-language-model (LLM)-assisted extraction pass with audit-trail merging of every revision, mirroring the dual-extraction discipline of systematic reviews~\cite{whiting2011quadas2}. A 20\% random sample (254 studies) was independently re-extracted by a second implementation to estimate inter-extractor agreement: Cohen's kappa for categorical fields and an intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of 0.930 on the continuous sigma-relevance rating.

\subsection{Quality Assessment}
Quality was assessed as a credibility signal, not an exclusion criterion: no study was removed from the review on quality grounds, consistent with scoping-review practice and PRISMA-ScR item 12~\cite{tricco2018prismascr}. Each study was scored on eight weighted dimensions (D1--D8)---venue tier, author authority, formal-methods rigour, empirical reproducibility, argumentative rigour, citation uptake, transparency, and prior-literature engagement---using a paper-type-adjusted rubric adapted from QUADAS-2, ROBINS-I, and PROBAST+AI for the machine-learning context~\cite{whiting2011quadas2,sterne2016robinsi,moons2025probastaialpha}. Scripted rules scored the venue- and citation-based dimensions; an LLM-assisted rating pass scored the remaining dimensions, with a 20\% independent-rater validation sample yielding per-dimension kappa values of 0.924--1.000 and a composite ICC of 0.947. Composite scores were mapped to tiers A--E (A = 2, B = 315, C = 826, D = 123, E = 2), where A--B denote high credibility, C moderate, and D--E low. Because quality was a credibility signal, a sensitivity analysis compared conclusions on the full set against the high-credibility subset (tiers A+B, n = 317): the prevalence of the sigma-trap signal was 46.3\% in the full set and 50.2\% in the subset, indicating that the headline conclusions are robust to credibility filtering. The rubric and full per-study scores are provided as supplementary material and summarised in Appendix~\ref{app:quality}.

\subsection{Synthesis}
The synthesis was descriptive and thematic, consistent with the guidance for synthesis without meta-analysis in scoping reviews~\cite{campbell2020swim,harrison2017albatross} and with GRADE-informed reporting of confidence in the evidence~\cite{guyatt2008grade}. The descriptive strand summarised the corpus by publication type, subdomain, year, venue, methodology, and formal content, addressing SQ1 and SQ2. The thematic strand proceeded by open coding of the charted text fields, grouping open codes into 13 axial themes, and mapping themes to the thesis-relevant constructs of schema coherence and the sigma-trap, addressing SQ3--SQ5. Theme membership was operationalised as keyword rules over the charted fields so that the synthesis is reproducible; theme sizes are indicative, and the full charted data, theme statistics, and rules are provided as supplementary material. Where quantitative claims about credibility or prevalence are made, they are reported with the quality assessment as context rather than as weighted evidence, reflecting the credibility-signal design of the quality assessment.

\subsection{Data Quality and Missing Data}
The charting pipeline included explicit quality checks. The key-contribution field was completed for 1,057 of 1,268 studies (83.4\%); the remainder were charted on a metadata basis and contribute to the descriptive statistics but not to the thematic coding, a limitation reported in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}. Controlled-vocabulary fields (subdomains, methodology, formal framework) passed automated consistency checks with zero violations after normalisation, and numerical fields (year, relevance, citation count) passed range checks. Missing data were handled by field: the 14 studies without a year are excluded only from time-series analyses; the 213 metadata-basis studies are retained for coverage and flagged throughout the synthesis. The 20\% validation sample (254 studies) provides the quantitative bound on charting reliability (ICC 0.930 on the continuous relevance rating), and the full data-quality report is provided as supplementary material.

The review included 1,268 studies. By publication type, empirical studies were most common (526; 41.5\%), followed by other (375; 29.6\%), position papers (131; 10.3\%), theoretical studies (123; 9.7\%), reviews (85; 6.7\%), and opinion pieces (28; 2.2\%). Methodologically, 787 studies (62.1\%) had no applicable empirical methodology recorded; experiments were reported in 367 (28.9\%), simulations in 58 (4.6\%), analyses in 33 (2.6\%), theory in 18 (1.4\%), and case studies in 5 (0.4\%). Quality assessment scored all 1,268 studies (median composite 2.15, IQR 1.94--2.35): 2 tier-A, 315 tier-B, 826 tier-C, 123 tier-D, and 2 tier-E studies, giving a high-credibility subset (tiers A+B) of 317 studies (25.0\%); 125 studies (9.9\%) were flagged low-credibility. Of the 1,268 studies, 587 (46.3\%) were sigma-trap relevant per the charted signal (core relevance $\geq$ 4: 510 studies, 40.2\%). The quality distribution is provided in full as supplementary material.

Methodology varied substantially by subdomain, and the variation is itself informative about where the field's empirical grounding sits. Value alignment, the largest subdomain, is only a third empirical (380 of 1,143 studies; 33.2\%), with the remainder split between conceptual analysis and position pieces; interpretability and robustness are the most empirical large subdomains (39.7\% and 39.0\%); governance is the least empirical of all (27.5\%). Formal content follows a different gradient: capability and control work carries the highest credibility and the most formal frameworks, while the evaluation and measurement theme---the second-largest theme in the corpus (281 studies)---is simultaneously the least formal and the least credible (17.8\% tier A+B). In other words, the field's empirical engine (evaluation, benchmarks) is its least trustworthy component, while its most credible component (governance, formal theory) is its least empirical one.

Credibility also varies by evidence basis: studies charted from full text (670) and from abstracts (385) dominate the high-credibility tiers, while the 213 metadata-basis studies---included for coverage---concentrate in the low tiers and contribute little to the thematic synthesis. The 20\% validation sample confirmed that the credibility rubric was applied consistently (per-dimension kappa 0.924--1.000, composite ICC 0.947), so the tier distribution is a property of the corpus rather than of the scoring process.

\subsection{Results by Subdomain}
\textbf{Value alignment} (1,143; 90.1\%) was the ubiquitous framing, co-occurring with every named subdomain in at least 92\% of cases, and growing from 1 study in 2015 to 354 in 2025. Its literature is dominated by three themes: proxy-reward failures (the reward-hacking theme; e.g., the formal characterisation of reward hacking in~\cite{P688}), preference and value learning, and ethics. Empirically, 380 studies (33.2\%) reported experimental or simulation methods, and credibility sat at the corpus baseline (25.7\% tier A+B).

\textbf{Ethics} (671; 52.9\%) was the second-largest subdomain, with a strong moral-philosophy strand (the ethics theme is the largest of the 13, at 333 studies) and growth from 0 studies in 2015--2016 to 212 in 2025. It was among the least empirical subdomains (210 studies; 31.3\% empirical) yet above baseline credibility (30.1\% tier A+B).

\textbf{Robustness} (605; 47.7\%) comprised security and adversarial work (jailbreaks, poisoning, prompt injection) and proxy-reward robustness; it grew from 6 studies in 2017 to 200 in 2025, with 236 studies (39.0\%) empirical. Robustness coupled strongly with the reward-hacking theme (Jaccard 0.200), reflecting the field's treatment of proxy misspecification as a robustness problem. Credibility was 28.8\%.

\textbf{Capabilities and control} (450; 35.5\%) covered the control problem, corrigibility (20 studies), and existential risk, growing from 1 study in 2015 to 137 in 2025. It had the highest credibility among the large subdomains (28.4\% tier A+B, including both tier-A studies in the corpus), with its theme---capabilities, control, and existential risk (200 studies)---at 43.5\% tier A+B.

\textbf{Interpretability} (390; 30.8\%) covered mechanistic analysis, feature attribution, and probing, growing from 2 studies in 2017 to 135 in 2026, with 155 studies (39.7\%) empirical. Its theme-level credibility was the lowest of any theme (11.7\% tier A+B for interpretability and mechanistic analysis, 77 studies), and subdomain-level credibility was 25.4\%, indicating a large but weakly validated measurement literature.

\textbf{Governance} (320; 25.2\%) covered oversight, regulation, and sociotechnical framing, including principal-agent analyses of misalignment~\cite{P007} and incentive-compatibility formulations of the alignment problem~\cite{P128}. It was the least empirical subdomain (88 studies; 27.5\%), yet had the highest subdomain-level credibility (35.0\% tier A+B), with the governance theme at 41.7\%---the credibility of the field's conceptual and regulatory analysis outstrips its empirical grounding.

\textbf{Mesa-optimization} (82; 6.5\%) was the smallest thesis-critical subdomain: 29 studies (35.4\%) empirical, growing from 2 in 2018 to 24 in 2026. Deceptive-alignment and sycophancy work concentrated here and carried high credibility (42.4\% tier A+B), including explicit monitoring proposals for deceptive alignment~\cite{P1097}; despite this, the subdomain remains thinly covered relative to its role in alignment failure.

\subsection{Cross-Cutting Themes}
Thematic analysis grouped open codes into 13 axial themes (Table~\ref{tab:themes}). All themes spanned at least three subdomains; their couplings define four clusters.

The \emph{reward-hacking cluster} linked proxy-reward and reward-hacking work (206 studies), preference and value learning (146), and robustness (166): the strongest couplings in the corpus (Jaccard 0.214 and 0.200). Proxy misspecification was the fastest-growing theme, from 4 studies in 2023 to 83 in 2025, and it is the connective tissue between alignment, robustness, and capabilities in the empirical literature.

The \emph{thesis cluster} linked internal representation structure and schema (104 studies) with compositional generalization and the $\sigma$-trap (178; coupling 0.195). Representation structure was discussed operationally---features, activations, latents---rather than axiomatically: 203 studies (16.0\%) discussed internal representations, but only 16 used the phrase ``internal representation'' literally, and the theme sits slightly above baseline credibility (23.1\% and 31.5\% tier A+B respectively).

The \emph{mechanism cluster} coupled interpretability and mechanistic analysis (77 studies) with the thesis cluster (0.153): mechanistic work is where representation structure is actually examined. The \emph{society cluster} coupled governance (120) with capabilities and control (200; 0.151) and ethics (333; 0.121): the risk framing runs from technical control into sociotechnical governance.

Two structural observations follow from the theme structure. First, credibility is not uniform across themes: the theoretically framed strands (formal methods 44.5\%, capabilities and control 43.5\%, governance 41.7\%, deceptive alignment 42.4\%) carry roughly twice the high-credibility share of the empirical strands (proxy reward 17.5\%, evaluation 17.8\%, interpretability 11.7\%). The field's most-cited and most-trusted work is its conceptual and formal work, while its empirical engine is its least-trusted component---an inversion that the quality assessment makes visible and that any synthesis must respect. Second, the thesis-relevant themes (internal representation structure, compositional generalization and the $\sigma$-trap) sit between these poles (23.1\% and 31.5\%), confirming that the structural literature is neither the strongest nor the weakest part of the corpus, but the least connected to the rest of it.

\subsection{Gaps}
The charted data confirmed all five gaps identified in the project's earlier gap analysis and surfaced three new ones. (i) \emph{Internal representation structure as a safety property} (G1): 203 studies discussed internal representations, of which 115 carried a formal framework, but the vocabulary of structure---``schema'' in 50 studies, ``compositionality'' in 0---was not axiomatised as a safety property. (ii) \emph{Compositional-generalisation-to-alignment bridge} (G2): ``compositional generalization'' appeared in 48 studies, ``goal misgeneralization'' in none, and the intersection with $\sigma$-trap language was 8 studies. (iii) \emph{Dynamical-systems safety treatment} (G3): 18 studies (1.4\%) used a dynamical-systems framework. (iv) \emph{Schema theory} (G4): 90 studies (7.1\%) discussed a concept related to schema coherence; 15 used the phrase literally. (v) \emph{Secondary gaps} (G5): continual-learning/forgetting vocabulary appeared in 3 studies and ``simplicity prior'' in none. Three new gaps emerged from the charted data: evaluation infrastructure quality (the evaluation theme, 281 studies, had 17.8\% tier A+B and included critiques of static-benchmark leakage~\cite{P864}), governance empirics (27.5\% empirical), and deceptive-alignment detection (33 studies, 2.6\% of the corpus despite 42.4\% tier A+B). Together these confirm the gap structure of the thesis: the ingredients of a schema-coherence account are present in the corpus, but the joints---the connecting concepts and the empirical bridge between them---are missing.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
% ============================================================

This scoping review mapped the landscape of AGI safety research as represented by 1,268 studies published between 2015 and 2026. The results describe a field that is large, recent, and skewed: 71.8\% of the corpus appeared in 2024--2026; value alignment is the near-universal framing (90.1\% of studies), while mesa-optimization, the subdomain closest to the thesis's structural failure mode, accounts for 6.5\%. The literature is dominated by empirical work (41.5\% of studies by type) that reports point estimates without uncertainty quantification, and the credibility assessment---used as a signal, not a filter---found that only 25.0\% of studies reach the high-credibility tiers.

The most consequential finding for the thesis project concerns the joints between the field's parts. The corpus contains the ingredients of a schema-coherence account of safety---203 studies (16.0\%) discuss internal representations, 587 (46.3\%) are sigma-trap relevant, and 48 studies discuss compositional generalization---but the connecting vocabulary is nearly absent: ``goal misgeneralization'' appears in no charted record, ``compositionality'' in none, and the intersection between compositional-generalization and sigma-trap language is 8 studies. The two literatures that the thesis seeks to unify, compositional generalisation and alignment, do not currently speak to each other in the published record. This is consistent with the project's gap analysis (G1--G5) and with the framing of the $\sigma$-trap as a missing bridge rather than a missing component~\cite{amsyar2026sigma}.

The review's findings align with, and extend, the existing review literature, but each prior review leaves a specific residue that this one addresses. Triantafyllopoulos et al.~\cite{triantafyllopoulos2026value} reviewed value alignment in advisory AI (83 studies) and concluded that fairness and cognition are underdeveloped and that evaluation is non-standardised; our corpus confirms both---the evaluation theme is the least credible large strand (17.8\% tier A+B) and the human-AI interaction theme (117 studies) is small relative to its role---but we additionally locate these weaknesses within a full subdomain map rather than within a single application domain. Shen et al.~\cite{shen2024bidirectional} argued that alignment is treated too statically and unidirectionally (400+ studies); our co-occurrence and theme data corroborate the narrowness (value alignment co-occurs with every other subdomain at $\geq$ 92\%, making it the default framing), while the governance theme's credibility premium (41.7\%) suggests the sociotechnical dimension they call for is emerging but empirically thin. Gyevnar and Kasirzadeh~\cite{gyevnar2025aisafety} explicitly flagged preprint and forum under-coverage; our inclusion of the grey-literature half of the corpus (arXiv alone 18.0\%, plus forum and workshop sources) is the direct response, and our venue-country proxy quantifies how concentrated that grey layer is in global preprint platforms. The earlier AGI-risk reviews~\cite{hendrycks2023overview} predate the empirical wave that dominates our 2024--2026 strata, so they cannot speak to the credibility distribution we report. Across all four comparisons, the consistent picture is that the field's fragmentation is not merely topical but structural---subdomains, methods, and credibility are unevenly distributed in ways that single-domain reviews cannot see.

\subsection{Answers to the Review Questions}
The five sub-questions receive explicit answers. \textbf{SQ1 (taxonomy)}: the literature exhibits a stable eight-subdomain taxonomy dominated by value alignment (90.1\%), with mesa-optimization and governance as the thin tails (6.5\% and 25.2\%). \textbf{SQ2 (formal methods)}: 63.9\% of studies are formal under a permissive definition, concentrated in optimization, probability, and logic, with dynamical-systems formalism nearly absent (1.4\%). \textbf{SQ3 (internal representation structure)}: a substantial minority (16.0\%) discusses internal representations, but the treatment is operational rather than axiomatic, and the schema-coherence vocabulary is essentially absent. \textbf{SQ4 (CG--alignment relationship)}: the compositional-generalisation and alignment literatures barely intersect (8 studies share both vocabularies; ``goal misgeneralization'' appears in none). \textbf{SQ5 (schema-coherence gaps)}: five pre-specified gaps are confirmed and three new ones identified, all traceable to charted data. Each answer is bounded by the review's credibility distribution: the strongest answers (SQ1, SQ2) rest on the full corpus, while the structural answers (SQ3--SQ5) rest on a minority of studies whose credibility is, at best, moderate.

Three findings carry implications for research practice. First, evaluation infrastructure is the largest low-credibility strand: the evaluation theme (281 studies) has the second-largest membership but only 17.8\% high-credibility tiers, and the corpus itself contains critiques of static-benchmark leakage~\cite{P864}. The field's measurement base needs methodological investment. Second, governance---the highest-credibility subdomain (35.0\%)---is the least empirical (27.5\%), meaning its claims rest on conceptual analysis; sociotechnical and principal-agent framings~\cite{P007,P128} would benefit from empirical evaluation. Third, deceptive-alignment detection is small (33 studies) despite high credibility, and mesa-optimization remains thinly covered (6.5\%) relative to its role in alignment failure.

These results bound the claims the thesis can make. They do not establish the $\sigma$-trap thesis; they establish that its ingredients are present in the literature, that the joints are missing, and that the field's evidence base is uneven in ways that subsequent work---including this project's systematic review, conceptual framework, and final synthesis---must respect~\cite{amsyar2026sigma}. For the broader field, the map's value is comparative: it provides the credibility-stratified baseline against which future reviews, including the project's own final scoping review (Paper 09), can measure the field's evolution, and it identifies where the field's self-correction mechanisms are weakest---evaluation infrastructure, governance empirics, and structural measurement.

\subsection{Limitations}
Several limitations qualify the findings. First, the review was conducted by a single reviewer, including screening, charting, and thematic coding; dual-reviewer screening and dual-coder thematic analysis were not performed, and the thematic synthesis relies on keyword-rule operationalizations whose sizes are indicative rather than exact. Second, the field moves quickly: the corpus is a snapshot (searches run July--August 2026) in a literature whose volume doubled in two years, and the 2026 strata are partial-year counts. Third, grey literature is included by design (arXiv alone is 18.0\% of the corpus), which broadens coverage but complicates venue-based credibility assessment and introduces coverage bias toward sources indexed by the included platforms---forum posts outside the AI Alignment Forum and preprint servers outside arXiv are under-represented. Fourth, the geographic analysis uses a venue-country proxy covering 48.4\% of studies and does not attribute authors' institutions, so the geography findings are indicative of publication provenance, not research capacity. Fifth, quality was assessed as a credibility signal with LLM-assisted scoring; the validation sample showed strong agreement (kappa 0.924--1.000, ICC 0.947), but the approach cannot substitute for full dual-reviewer critical appraisal. Sixth, 213 studies (16.8\%) were charted on a metadata basis and contribute little free text to the thematic analysis. Seventh, the thematic coding is a single-coder interpretation operationalised as keyword rules: it is reproducible but reflects one analytical stance, and theme boundaries are conventional. Finally, the LLM-assisted extraction pass introduces a tool-specific variability that the 20\% validation sample (ICC 0.930) bounds but does not eliminate.

\subsection{Relation to Other Chapters}
This review is Paper 01 of a thesis-by-publication project. Its results feed the subsequent papers as follows. Paper 02, the systematic review of the $\sigma$-trap, inherits the credibility distribution (a high-credibility subset of 317 studies and a sigma-trap signal of 587 studies) and the compositional-generalisation subset (48 studies) as its evidence base. Paper 03, the conceptual framework, targets the gaps documented here---schema theory, dynamical-systems treatment, and the CG--alignment bridge. Paper 09, the final scoping review, will report the field's evolution against this baseline and the credibility-based appraisal of its evidence base. The present review's single-reviewer and LLM-assisted methodology bounds the strength of the evidence map it provides to these papers.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================

The research question posed in the introduction---\emph{how is the landscape of AGI safety research structured in terms of subdomains, formal methods, and the treatment of internal representation structure, and what gaps exist that a schema-coherence framework could address?}---receives the following answer. The landscape is structured as a value-alignment-centred, empirically dominated, credibility-skewed corpus: eight subdomains, thirteen themes, and a formalism gradient that runs from optimization and probability at the empirical core to an almost absent dynamical-systems treatment at the structural margin. Internal representation structure is discussed by a substantial minority of studies but never axiomatised as a safety property, and the compositional-generalisation and alignment literatures do not currently connect. Five pre-specified gaps are confirmed and three new ones identified. The review's contribution is this integrated, protocol-driven map and its gap structure---an incremental but necessary step within the thesis programme, which does not claim to establish the schema-coherence thesis it serves.

Four conclusions follow. First, the field is young and rapidly growing, dominated by value alignment and empirical evaluation, with credibility concentrated in theoretical and governance work. Second, the literature exhibits a structural gap that a schema-coherence framework could address: internal representation structure is discussed by a substantial minority of studies, but the connecting concepts---schema coherence, compositional generalization, goal misgeneralization---are almost entirely absent from the corpus. Third, the field's evidence base is uneven: evaluation infrastructure is large but low-credibility, governance is high-credibility but non-empirical, and deceptive-alignment detection is under-developed. Fourth, the thesis's claim that compositional generalisation failure and alignment failure are the same phenomenon is neither established nor contradicted by this corpus; the review establishes that the ingredients are present and the joints are missing.

\subsection{Recommendations}
Three recommendations follow, each tied to a finding and specified as to what, who, and how. First, the field's measurement base requires methodological investment: benchmark and evaluation designers (the evaluation theme's 281 studies) should adopt uncertainty quantification---confidence intervals and seed-level reporting---as a reporting norm, following the credibility findings (only 25.0\% of studies reach the high-credibility tiers, and the evaluation theme is at 17.8\%). Second, governance research needs an empirical bridge: governance researchers should test principal-agent and incentive-compatibility claims~\cite{P007,P128} against deployed systems and evaluation data, because the highest-credibility subdomain (35.0\%) is the least empirical (27.5\%). Third, the structural measurement agenda that the thesis pursues is timely: researchers studying OOD generalization should report internal-structure diagnostics alongside behavioural metrics, which is the concrete step that would begin to close the G1--G4 gaps (16.0\% of studies already discuss internal representations; the missing piece is standardised measurement). For future research, the priorities are an empirical programme for schema-coherence measurement, a joint compositional-generalisation--alignment benchmark, and a credibility-stratified re-analysis of the field's headline claims.
