Background: AGI safety has grown into a large, fast-moving field, but existing reviews are fragmented and under-cover the preprint and forum literature where much of the field develops. Whether the compositional-generalisation and alignment literatures are connected remains open.

Objectives: To map the English-language AGI safety literature (2015–2026, peer-reviewed and grey) into a subdomain taxonomy, methods catalogue, and treatment of internal representation; to locate where compositional generalisation and alignment research intersect; and to identify evidence gaps relevant to schema-coherence accounts.

Methods: We conducted a PRISMA-ScR scoping review across six databases plus grey literature. Of 5,191 records identified, 2,867 were screened; 1,268 records (1,136 unique studies) were included. Structured fields were charted by a hybrid pipeline (validation ICC 0.930); credibility was scored on an eight-dimension rubric (validation kappa 0.924–1.000); open coding yielded 13 axial themes.

Results: The corpus grew sharply (72.6% from 2024–2026). Value alignment dominated (89.8%); mesa-optimization was thin (6.4%). Only 24.7% reached the high-credibility tiers; evaluation work was large but low-credibility (251 studies; 17.9%). 15.5% of studies discussed internal representations; 40.8% carried a researcher-defined heuristic schema-coherence signal (a coding rule, not endorsement), and only 8 studies used both vocabularies—a vocabulary finding.

Conclusions: The AGI safety landscape is mapped: the ingredients of a schema-coherence account are present, explicit connections between these literatures remain limited, and the evidence base is uneven: the largest empirical strand (evaluation) is the least credible, and the most credible strands are the least empirical. These findings provide a baseline for testing schema-coherence accounts of the generalisation–alignment relationship.
