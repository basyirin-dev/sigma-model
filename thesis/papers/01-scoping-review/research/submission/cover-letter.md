Re: Submission of "Mapping the Landscape of AGI Safety: A Scoping Review of Subdomains, Methods, and Internal Representation"

Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit the above manuscript for consideration as a review article in Artificial Intelligence Review. This is a PRISMA-ScR scoping review, single-authored, with a prospectively registered protocol.

The review provides the first credibility-stratified map of the AGI-safety literature: 1,136 unique studies (from 1,268 records screened across six databases plus grey literature, with duplicate preprint/published version pairs collapsed) are organised into an eight-subdomain taxonomy, a methods-and-formalism census, and a treatment of internal-representation structure — the three elements no prior review integrates (the closest predecessors map at most one). Its headline finding is a credibility inversion: the field's largest empirical strand, evaluation and benchmarking (251 studies), carries the lowest credibility (17.9% tier A+B), while its most credible strands, governance and formal theory, are the least empirical (33.7% and 1.1% empirical respectively). A structural vocabulary gap is mapped with appropriate caveats: only 44 studies use compositional-generalisation vocabulary and 8 intersect with σ-trap language — reported as a vocabulary finding, not a claim about the field's conceptual connections.

The manuscript fits Artificial Intelligence Review's scope directly: the journal regularly publishes systematic and scoping reviews of AI research, and a reproducible evidence map of a field whose volume doubled in two years (72.6% of the corpus published 2024–2026) is timely and field-agnostic.

For transparency: the protocol was registered prospectively on the Open Science Framework (osf.io/ntuh2) with no amendments, and the review is reported against the 22-item PRISMA-ScR checklist. Charting used a hybrid pipeline (scripted extraction plus an LLM-assisted pass with an audit-trail merge), validated on a 20% random sample (ICC 0.930; per-dimension kappa 0.924–1.000), with a human-adjudicated spot-check of charted records against full texts (80–93% agreement across four high-inference fields) and a search-sensitivity analysis showing the headline results do not depend on the targeted search families. A duplicate-version reconciliation (132 collapsed pairs) and the full reconciliation log accompany the supplementary data.

The work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and I declare no competing interests and no external funding. Schema coherence is used strictly as a heuristic search lens, not as a conclusion of this review.

Yours sincerely,
Basyirin Amsyar Basri
