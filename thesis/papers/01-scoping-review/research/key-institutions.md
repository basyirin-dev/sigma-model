# Institutional Mapping and Strategic Dynamics of AGI Safety Research

The institutional landscape of artificial general intelligence (AGI) safety research has undergone a rapid structural evolution, transitioning from a pre-paradigmatic, highly philosophical academic field into an intense, empirically driven scientific discipline shaped by commercial frontier laboratories, academic foundations, and specialized non-profit organizations. As artificial intelligence models transition from text-generating tools to autonomous agentic systems, the methodologies and strategic alignments within the safety community have fractured. This report provides an exhaustive analysis of the key organizations, internal groups, research paradigms, and inter-organizational dynamics that define the modern AGI safety ecosystem.

## Legacy Foundations: Philosophical and Theoretical Pioneers

The mathematical and conceptual foundations of advanced AI risk mitigation originated in specialized research centers established to analyze long-term existential threats and formulate rational agent constraints before the deep learning scaling boom.

FHI pioneered the academic study of existential risk, longtermism, and the ethical status of digital minds. Founded and directed by Nick Bostrom, it was designed to shield researchers from standard academic pressures. Over its nineteen-year history, FHI secured over $20 million in grants from Open Philanthropy and additional support from the Survival and Flourishing Fund. The University of Oxford closed the institute on April 16, 2024, after severe administrative tensions with the Faculty of Philosophy, which had imposed a freeze on hiring and external fundraising in 2020. Former senior research fellow Anders Sandberg described the closure as "gradual suffocation by Faculty bureaucracy."

The Technical Governance Team has shifted toward strategic macrostrategy, arguing for a coordinated international moratorium on frontier AI development.

## Technical Synthesis of Advanced Alignment Methods

The core challenges in AGI safety can be organized into three strategic domains: creating trustworthy systems (Development), evaluating capability and alignment risks (Assessment), and monitoring systems after deployment (Control).

```
                  ┌──────────────────────────────┐
                  │      DEVELOPMENT STAGE       │
                  │  • CIRL preference learning  │
                  │  • Weak-to-strong feedback   │
                  └──────────────┬───────────────┘
                                 │
                                 ▼
                  ┌──────────────────────────────┐
                  │       ASSESSMENT STAGE       │
                  │  • Sparse Autoencoders (SAE) │
                  │  • Dangerous capability evals│
                  └──────────────┬───────────────┘
                                 │
                                 ▼
                  ┌──────────────────────────────┐
                  │        CONTROL STAGE         │
                  │  • Real-time supervisions    │
                  │  • Off-switch shutdown games │
                  └──────────────────────────────┘
```

**Development — Preference Learning (CIRL):** Formalized as a game of incomplete information between a human and an agent. The human knows the true reward function $\theta \in \Theta$; the agent holds a prior belief distribution over possible human values, optimizing cumulative reward while updating beliefs based on human instruction:

$$\max_{\pi} \mathbb{E}_{H, A} \left[ \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \gamma^t R(s_t, a_t; \theta) \right]$$

**Development — Weak-to-Strong Supervision:** A weaker teacher model $\mathbf{T}_{weak}$ generates labels to train a stronger student $\mathbf{S}_{strong}$. Performance Gap Recovered (PGR) measures how closely the weakly supervised strong model approaches its maximum potential:

$$PGR = \frac{\text{Acc}(\mathbf{S}_{strong}) - \text{Acc}(\mathbf{T}_{weak})}{\text{Acc}(\mathbf{S}_{optimal}) - \text{Acc}(\mathbf{T}_{weak})}$$

**Assessment — Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs):** Decompose internal representations into interpretable features by projecting model activations $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ into an overcomplete sparse bottleneck $\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^m$ ($m \gg d$):

$$\mathbf{\hat{x}} = \mathbf{W}_{dec} \cdot \mathbf{h}(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{b}_{dec}$$

Sparsity is enforced via Top-K gating or $L_1$ penalty:

$$\mathcal{L} = \left\| \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{\hat{x}} \right\|_2^2 + \lambda \sum_{i=1}^m |h_i|$$

**Control — Off-Switch Game:** Models how an agent chooses whether to allow human shutdown. Under preference uncertainty, the agent defers to human control because it recognizes the human will only trigger shutdown if the agent's planned action is misaligned with the human's true utility $U(a)$.

## Strategic Analysis: The Conflict of Paradigms

The institutional landscape of AGI safety is divided by fundamental conflicts regarding the nature of intelligence, takeoff speeds, and the tractability of safety engineering.

**Prosaic Alignment ("Prosers"):** Researchers at Anthropic, ARC, and DeepMind operate under a "slow takeoff" assumption — AI progress is gradual and smooth, with each capability increase commercially deployed. Safety teams run empirical evaluations on real-world systems highly similar to future AGI platforms, scaling empirical techniques like sparse autoencoders and weak-to-strong supervision.

**Agent Foundations ("Poets"):** MIRI and Conjecture assume a "fast takeoff" — once a model reaches qualitative generality, recursive self-improvement compresses the transition to superintelligence into weeks or days. Empirical iteration on current models is futile as the architecture will undergo a "sharp left turn," and safety metrics will suffer from extreme Goodharting. Alignment must be solved via formal mathematical logic beforehand, or development halted entirely through international policy.

**Real-World Impact — The SB 1047 Debate:** The failed 2024 California SB 1047 bill sought mandatory pre-deployment audits, third-party compliance verification, and "kill switch" mechanisms. Proponents (FLI, CAIS, CHAI) argued computing power and training cost are reliable regulatory thresholds. Opponents argued compute-based regulation is arbitrary, would stifle open-source innovation, and fails to account for smaller optimized models.

**The AI Control Shift:** A strategic shift from assuming models can be made inherently aligned to designing robust containment frameworks. Pioneered by Redwood Research and DeepMind, this paradigm accepts that frontier agents may be untrusted and deceptively aligned. Instead of relying on values-based training, control structures deploy dumber-but-trusted supervisor models to continuously monitor planning transcripts, intercept API calls, and audit changes — a pragmatic response to shortening capability horizons and commercial competitive pressures.
