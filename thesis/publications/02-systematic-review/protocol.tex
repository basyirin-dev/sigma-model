\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background:} Neural networks exhibit a persistent failure mode known as the $\sigma$-trap (schema-coherence trap), wherein models with low schema coherence ($\sigma$) catastrophically fail on out-of-distribution (OOD) compositional generalisation tasks, despite high in-distribution accuracy. While numerous narrative surveys have catalogued shortcut learning, domain generalisation, and compositional learning, none have systematically quantified the $\sigma$-trap's prevalence or the comparative efficacy of $\sigma$-targeting interventions. This protocol describes a systematic review and meta-analysis to fill that gap.

\textbf{Methods:} We will search Scopus, Web of Science, ACM DL, IEEE Xplore, arXiv, and PsycINFO from 2017 to 2026 for empirical studies of neural network compositional generalisation that report in-distribution and OOD accuracy. Two independent reviewers will screen records, extract data using a structured form (80 fields, controlled vocabularies), and assess risk of bias using the $\sigma$-ROB tool (six domains adapted from QUADAS-2, ROBINS-I, and PROBAST+AI). The primary effect size is the log odds ratio of correct classification. A three-level random-effects meta-analysis with robust variance estimation will be used if $k \geq 10$ studies are eligible; otherwise a structured narrative synthesis will be conducted.

\textbf{Registration:} OSF \url{https://osf.io/m3asw} (immutable registration); PROSPERO not applicable (non-medical systematic review of computer science literature).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Rationale (Item 5)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

Neural networks achieve high in-distribution (ID) accuracy across diverse tasks, yet often fail catastrophically on out-of-distribution (OOD) compositional generalisation --- the ability to recombine known components in novel configurations. This failure, termed the $\sigma$-trap (schema-coherence trap), is characterised by low schema coherence ($\sigma$): the model's internal representations lack the structured, compositional organisation that supports systematic generalisation~\cite{lake2018generalization, keysers2020measuring, huvard2023notion}.

A review of the existing review literature (14 surveys and meta-reviews) reveals critical gaps. Geirhos et al.~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut} identified shortcut learning as a pervasive failure mode but provided no quantitative synthesis. Hupkes et al.~\cite{hupkes2023taxonomy} conducted an extensive review of over 700 experiments with a five-axis taxonomy but scoped their analysis to NLP and performed no meta-analysis. Matta et al.~\cite{matta2024systematic} conducted a PRISMA-style systematic review of domain generalisation methods for medical imaging (77 articles) but restricted to a single database and medical imaging. Sinha et al.~\cite{sinha2024systematic} provided the broadest coverage of compositional learning but noted the ``lack of systematic theoretical and experimental research methodologies'' and performed no quantitative aggregation. The remaining reviews --- by Liu et al.~\cite{liu2021towards}, Zhou et al.~\cite{zhou2023domain}, Wang et al.~\cite{wang2022generalizing}, Karbasian et al.~\cite{karbasian2025unified}, and others --- are narrative surveys without pooled effect sizes or intervention meta-analysis.

\noindent\textbf{No existing review:}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Quantifies the prevalence or magnitude of the ID-OOD generalisation gap across benchmarks and architectures;
\item Compares the efficacy of $\sigma$-targeting interventions (e.g., SAM, SWAD, meta-learning, representation regularisation) against standard training;
\item Applies a formal risk of bias assessment tailored to computational ML experiments.
\end{enumerate}

Our preliminary gap analysis identifies 11 studies with extractable ID-OOD numerics, showing a gap range of 22--98 percentage points across benchmark families (SCAN, COGS, CFQ, gSCAN, CLOSURE, PCFG-SET) and architectures (RNN, LSTM, GRU, Transformer, CNN-LSTM). The median gap of 60--85 points confirms that the $\sigma$-trap is a robust phenomenon, not an artifact of a single benchmark or model family. However, no systematic estimate of the pooled effect size exists, and the comparative efficacy of $\sigma$-targeting interventions has not been quantitatively synthesised.

\subsection{Objectives (Item 6)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection{Primary Research Question}

In studies of neural network models (P), do training or architectural interventions (I) compared with standard optimisation approaches (C) improve out-of-distribution compositional generalisation (O)?

\subsubsection{SMART Objectives}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Specific:} Systematically identify, appraise, and synthesise empirical studies of compositional generalisation in neural networks published between 2017 and 2026.
\item \textbf{Measurable:} Extract ID and OOD accuracy from eligible studies; compute log odds ratio (LOR) as the primary effect size; synthesise via three-level random-effects meta-analysis with robust variance estimation.
\item \textbf{Attainable:} Preliminary evidence suggests at least 20--30 studies have extractable ID-OOD pairs across eight benchmark families, sufficient for meta-analysis with subgroup comparisons ($k \geq 10$ per subgroup threshold).
\item \textbf{Relevant:} The $\sigma$-trap directly impacts the reliability and safety of neural network deployment, particularly in high-stakes domains where OOD generalisation is critical.
\item \textbf{Time-bound:} Screening completed by [Month Year]; extraction by [Month Year]; analysis and manuscript by [Month Year].
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Secondary Questions}

\begin{enumerate}[label=S\arabic*:, nosep]
\item How does $\sigma$-trap prevalence vary by architecture family (CNN, Transformer, MLP, GNN)?
\item Which $\sigma$-targeting interventions (SAM variants, SWAD, entropy-SGD, MESA, representation regularisation, data augmentation) show the largest pooled effect sizes?
\item How does model scale (parameter count) moderate the $\sigma$-trap severity?
\item Is the $\sigma$-trap distinct from shortcut learning in empirical manifestation?
\item What is the relationship between $\sigma$-trap severity and OOD shift type (compositional vs.\ covariate vs.\ concept vs.\ domain shift)?
\item Do studies with higher reproducibility standards (code availability, seed reporting) report different effect sizes?
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Secondary Questions for Discussion Only (No Independent Synthesis)}

\begin{enumerate}[label=D\arabic*:, nosep, start=7]
\item Is there evidence linking the $\sigma$-trap to alignment failures in AI safety (mesa-optimisation, specification gaming)?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Eligibility Criteria (Item 7)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection{Study Design}

\textbf{Inclusion:} Empirical studies evaluating neural network performance on compositional generalisation tasks.\ Studies must report both ID and OOD accuracy (or sufficient data to compute them). Randomised controlled trials (RCTs) and quasi-experimental designs are eligible.\ Studies using established compositional generalisation benchmarks (SCAN, COGS, CFQ, gSCAN, PCFG-SET, CoFe, SQOOP, CLOSURE) are included even if they use alternative terminology.\ Systematic reviews and meta-analyses are excluded (used for contextual reference).

\textbf{Exclusion:} Theoretical papers with no experiments; opinion pieces, perspectives, and position papers without empirical evaluation; dataset-only or benchmark-only papers without model evaluation; studies exclusively on non-compositional OOD tasks (simple covariate shift without compositional structure); \textbf{OOD detection / anomaly detection} studies (using ``OOD'' in the sense of detecting whether a single input is distributionally novel, not measuring generalisation to unseen compositional combinations); \textbf{neural closure models} for partial differential equations that use the term ``compositional'' in the context of function composition rather than linguistic/structural compositionality.

\subsubsection{Population (P)}

\textbf{Inclusion:} Neural network models of any architecture (CNN, RNN, LSTM, GRU, Transformer, ViT, MLP, GNN, neurosymbolic hybrid, differentiable tree machine, state-space model, structured state-space sequence model (S4, Mamba), mixture-of-experts) trained via gradient-based optimisation on supervised learning tasks.

\textbf{Exclusion:} Pure symbolic systems without neural components; evolutionary or genetic algorithm approaches; statistical models (SVM, random forest, logistic regression) without neural elements; unsupervised or reinforcement learning settings (captured in a secondary stream, see below).

\subsubsection{Intervention (I)}

\textbf{Inclusion:} Any training or architectural modification intended to improve compositional, systematic, or zero-shot generalisation performance, including but not limited to: sharpness-aware minimisation (SAM, ASAM, GSAM, F-SAM); stochastic weight averaging (SWAD, SWA); entropy-SGD; meta-learning for OOD generalisation; data augmentation that targets compositionality (e.g., compositional data augmentation, template-based augmentation); architectural modifications (e.g., attention, relative positional encodings, compositional attention, transformer depth/compositionality analyses); regularisation encouraging structured representations (e.g., coherence penalties, information bottleneck, variational dropout); and in-context learning strategies for compositional tasks (e.g., prompt selection, exemplar ordering).

\textbf{Exclusion:} Training modifications applied identically to both intervention and baseline conditions (must differ between conditions); interventions targeting non-OOD objectives (e.g., only ID accuracy, only training speed) with no clear link to OOD generalisation.

\subsubsection{Comparator (C)}

\textbf{Inclusion:} Standard empirical risk minimisation (ERM) or standard stochastic gradient descent (SGD) training on the same architecture, dataset, and split.\ Baseline must be trained on the same ID data as the intervention.

\textbf{Exclusion:} Comparisons without a clear baseline (e.g., reporting OOD accuracy alone without ID accuracy or an SGD baseline); comparisons where the baseline differs in architecture, training data, or data split from the intervention in non-causal ways.

\subsubsection{Outcome (O)}

\textbf{Inclusion:} Accuracy (top-1, exact match, or per-example accuracy) reported separately for ID and OOD splits.\ For established benchmarks (SCAN, COGS, CFQ, gSCAN, PCFG-SET), standard split definitions are used to identify ID/OOD grouping.\ The primary effect size is the log odds ratio (LOR) of correct classification computed from ID and OOD accuracy (see SAP-1).\ Secondary outcomes include F1-score, AUC-ROC, and per-split accuracy where available.

\textbf{Exclusion:} Studies reporting only aggregated metrics across ID and OOD; studies reporting only loss without accuracy; qualitative evaluations without quantitative outcome measures; studies where OOD accuracy cannot be assigned to individual OOD splits (i.e., only mean OOD accuracy across heterogeneous splits without per-split breakdown).

\subsubsection{Timing and Setting}

\textbf{Inclusion:} Studies published from January 2017 (the emergence of systematic compositional generalisation benchmarks) to the date of search execution.\ Any setting (academic, industrial, independent).\ Any language, provided the full text is available in English.

\textbf{Exclusion:} Studies published before 2017 (pre-SCAN era, where compositional benchmarks lacked systematic ID-OOD splits).

\subsection{Study Selection (Item 9)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection{Stage 1: Title and Abstract Screening}

Records from all databases will be merged and deduplicated in Zotero. Two independent reviewers will screen titles and abstracts against the eligibility criteria using Rayyan~\cite{ouzzani2016rayyan}.\ Conflicts will be resolved through discussion or a third reviewer.\ Reviewers will not be blinded to authors, institutions, or journals.\ Each record is classified as \textit{include}, \textit{exclude}, or \textit{uncertain}; uncertain records proceed to full-text screening.\ A pilot calibration screening of 50 titles will be conducted to ensure inter-reviewer agreement $\geq 80\%$ before full screening begins.

\subsubsection{Stage 2: Full-Text Screening}

Full texts of all included and uncertain records will be retrieved. Two reviewers independently assess eligibility with reasons for exclusion documented.\ Excluded studies at full-text stage are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:excluded} with specific reasons.\ Conflicts resolved via consensus or third reviewer.

\subsubsection{Stage 3: Pre-Registration of Data Extraction}

All studies passing full-text screening are registered in the extraction database before extraction begins, preventing selective reporting of extraction outcomes.

\subsection{Data Collection Process (Item 10)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection{Extraction Protocol}

Two independent reviewers will extract data from each included study using a standardised extraction form (80 fields with controlled vocabularies, validation rules, and mandatory fields).\ Extraction is performed in a pilot phase (5 papers) with inter-reviewer agreement assessment.\ Disagreements resolved by consensus or a third reviewer.

Extraction is performed study-level, not experiment-level.\ If a paper reports multiple experiments, the primary experiment (as defined by the authors or the most comprehensive evaluation) is coded; sub-experiments are recorded using linked sub-forms.\ Extraction is performed using a structured spreadsheet or database with validation rules (field types, controlled vocabularies, required fields).

\subsubsection{Missing Data}

If accuracy values are only available graphically, PlotDigitizer~\cite{plotdigitizer} will be used to extract numerical values with uncertainty bounds.\ If critical data are missing (e.g., OOD accuracy unreported, variance unreported), authors will be contacted once by email with a follow-up after two weeks.\ Data marked as \textit{unreported} will be coded as missing rather than imputed in the primary analysis.\ Sensitivity analyses will assess the impact of missing data (see SAP-5).

\subsection{Risk of Bias (Item 12)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection{$\sigma$-ROB Tool}

Risk of bias will be assessed using the $\sigma$-ROB tool, adapted from QUADAS-2~\cite{whiting2011quadas2}, ROBINS-I~\cite{sterne2016robinsi}, and PROBAST+AI~\cite{moons2025probastaialpha} for the specific context of ML experiments on the $\sigma$-trap.\ The tool comprises six domains:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Reproducibility}: Seeds, code, hyperparameters, dataset version, hardware
\item \textbf{Benchmark validity}: Appropriateness of the OOD benchmark, ID-OOD split design, benchmark contamination
\item \textbf{Confounding}: Architecture differences between intervention and comparator, training pipeline differences, data leakage
\item \textbf{Reporting completeness}: Selective outcome reporting, selective benchmark reporting, completeness of accuracy reporting
\item \textbf{Statistical rigour}: Seed variance reporting, statistical significance testing, multiple comparison correction
\item \textbf{External validity}: Range of benchmarks, range of architectures, reproducibility across settings
\end{enumerate}

Each domain is rated \textit{Low}, \textit{Unclear}, or \textit{High} risk of bias based on explicit signalling questions (3--6 per domain).\ The overall risk of bias is determined as follows: all domains Low $\rightarrow$ Low risk; one or more Unclear, no High $\rightarrow$ Some concerns; one or more High $\rightarrow$ High risk.\ Domain scores are not summed.

\subsubsection{Assessment Process}

Two independent reviewers assess each included study.\ Disagreements resolved by consensus or third reviewer.\ Reviewers pilot the tool on 3--5 studies before full assessment.\ Results will be presented as a summary table and traffic-light plot across all included studies.

\subsubsection{Narrative Synthesis}

If fewer than 10 studies are eligible, we will conduct a structured narrative synthesis following SWiM guidelines~\cite{campbell2020swim} with: grouping by intervention type and benchmark family; vote counting based on direction of effect (with explicit caveats about its limitations); an albatross plot~\cite{harrison2017albatross} for visual synthesis of effect size and precision.

\subsubsection{Meta-Analysis}

If $k \geq 10$ studies report extractable effect sizes, we will conduct a three-level random-effects meta-analysis.\ Details are provided in the Statistical Analysis Plan (SAP) below.

\subsection{Subgroup and Sensitivity Analyses (Item 13b)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection{Pre-Specified Subgroup Analyses}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Architecture family (CNN, ViT, MLP, GNN, neurosymbolic)
\item Intervention type (SAM, ASAM, SWAD, entropy-SGD, MESA, data augmentation, architectural)
\item Benchmark family (WILDS, domain generalisation, corruption, compositional, synthetic shift)
\item Model scale (small $<10$M, medium 10M--1B, large $>1$B parameters)
\item OOD shift severity (mild, moderate, severe, by ID-OOD gap)
\item Seed reporting ($\geq 3$ seeds, single seed, unspecified)
\item Preprint vs.\ peer-reviewed status
\end{enumerate}

Each subgroup is tested via mixed-effects meta-regression with robust variance estimation (RVE).\ Minimum $k \geq 10$ per subgroup for pooling; $k = 5$--9 reported with low-power caveat; $k < 5$ narrative only.

\subsubsection{Sensitivity Analyses}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Exclude high-risk-of-bias studies
\item Exclude preprints
\item Exclude single-seed studies
\item Exclude studies with data leakage flags
\item Restrict to externally validated studies
\item Restrict to studies with code/checkpoints available
\item Leave-one-out (study-level and benchmark-level)
\item Compare LOR-pooled vs.\ Hedges' g-pooled estimates
\end{enumerate}

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Meta-Bias Assessment (Item 14)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

Publication bias and selective reporting will be assessed using: contour-enhanced funnel plot (if $k \geq 10$); Egger's regression with RVE-based standard errors; p-uniform* for bias-adjusted pooled estimate; benchmark-selectivity audit (within-study ratio of reported-to-standard benchmarks); preprint vs.\ published subgroup comparison.\ All tests reported with the explicit caveat that funnel asymmetry in ML may reflect benchmark-selective reporting rather than study suppression.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Confidence in Cumulative Evidence (Item 15)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

The overall quality of the body of evidence will be assessed using GRADE~\cite{guyatt2008grade} adapted for computational ML experiments:\

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Risk of bias}: Based on $\sigma$-ROB assessment
\item \textbf{Inconsistency}: Based on $I^2$, prediction interval width, and overlap of subgroup estimates
\item \textbf{Indirectness}: Relevance of benchmarks and shift types to real-world OOD settings
\item \textbf{Imprecision}: Width of confidence and prediction intervals relative to meaningful effect thresholds (LOR = 0 corresponds to no effect; LOR > 1.0 considered meaningful)
\item \textbf{Publication bias}: Based on funnel plot, Egger's test, and benchmark-selectivity audit
\end{enumerate}

The evidence will be rated as High, Moderate, Low, or Very Low for each research question.\ Non-quantitative outcomes (secondary question D1) will not receive a GRADE rating.

\section{Statistical Analysis Plan}
% ============================================================

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Effect Size Definition (SAP-1)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{Primary effect size:} Log odds ratio (LOR) of correct classification, computed per study as:

\begin{align}
\text{LOR}_i &= \log\left( \frac{p_\sigma (1 - p_{\text{SGD}})}{p_{\text{SGD}} (1 - p_\sigma)} \right) \\
V_i &= \frac{1}{n_i \cdot p_\sigma (1 - p_\sigma)} + \frac{1}{n_i \cdot p_{\text{SGD}} (1 - p_{\text{SGD}})}
\end{align}

Continuity correction 0.5/n applied when $p = 0$ or $p = 1$.\
Back-transformed odds ratios and risk differences will be reported for interpretability.

\textbf{Secondary effect size:} Hedges' $g$ on seed-level accuracy, restricted to studies reporting $\geq 3$ seeds with standard deviation.\ Small-sample correction $J \approx 1 - 3/(4df - 1)$ applied.

\textbf{Tertiary (fallback):} Raw accuracy difference $\Delta\text{Acc}$ with binomial SE, pooled only within homogeneous benchmark families.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Pooling Model (SAP-2)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{Primary model:} Three-level random-effects model (effect sizes nested within studies, benchmark-cluster random effect):

\begin{verbatim}
rma.mv(yi, V, random = ~ 1 | study_id / es_id,
       struct = "ID", method = "REML", data = dat)
\end{verbatim}

All inference uses \texttt{clubSandwich::coef\_test} with the Tipton (2015) small-sample RVE correction.

\textbf{Secondary:} Pairwise random-effects (DerSimonian--Laird with HKSJ) on study-aggregated effects, as a sensitivity check.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Heterogeneity Assessment (SAP-3)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

Report $\tau^2$, $I^2$ (with CI), Cochran's $Q$ (with df and $p$), and the 95\% prediction interval.\
$I^2 > 75\%$ triggers mandatory subgroup/meta-regression exploration; it does not trigger abandonment of pooling.\
Leave-one-study-out influence analysis and Baujat plot.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Subgroup and Meta-Regression (SAP-4)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

Pre-specified moderators tested via mixed-effects meta-regression with RVE (report $Q_M$ for moderator, $Q_E$ for residual heterogeneity):

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Architecture family: CNN / ViT / MLP / other
\item Intervention type: SAM / ASAM / SWAD / entropy-SGD / MESA / other
\item Benchmark family: WILDS / DG-suite / corruption / compositional / synthetic
\item Model scale: $<10$M / 10M--1B / $>1$B params
\item OOD shift severity: baseline ID-OOD gap $<10\%$ / 10--25\% / $>25\%$
\item Perturbation radius $\rho$ (SAM-family): $<0.05$ / 0.05--0.2 / $>0.2$
\item External validation: yes / no
\item Seed reporting: $\geq 3$ seeds / single seed / unspecified
\item Preprint status: preprint / peer-reviewed
\end{enumerate}

Minimum $k \geq 10$ per subgroup for pooling; $k = 5$--9 reported with low-power caveat; $k < 5$ narrative only.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Sensitivity Analyses (SAP-5)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

Exclude high-ROB studies; exclude preprints; exclude single-seed studies; exclude data leakage; restrict to externally validated; restrict to code-available; leave-one-out (study and benchmark); LOR vs.\ Hedges' $g$ concordance check.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
