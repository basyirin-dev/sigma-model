\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Problem:} Agents trained to high in-distribution accuracy on
compositional benchmarks often fail near-completely on
out-of-distribution (OOD) recombination. We study this dissociation
with two quantities: parametric depth $\delta_A$ (fit to the training
distribution) and schema coherence $\sigma_A$ (organisation around the
task's compositional structure), which standard training does not
formally target.

\textbf{Method:} We posit a two-variable phenomenological ODE model of
training dynamics --- a depth-growth equation and an autocatalytic
schema-coherence equation. The model predicts a stable low-coherence
state (the $\sigma$-trap), a critical threshold
$\sigma_{\text{critical}}$ marking a transcritical bifurcation, and a
phase-2 acceleration in OOD accuracy. The mapping from SGD trajectories
to the ODEs is stated explicitly as Conjecture~\ref{conj:sgd-ode}, an
unproven modelling assumption: the model is posited, not derived from
gradient descent.

\textbf{Results:} In a four-arm experiment ($n = 15$ per arm, 2000
steps) on a SCAN-style benchmark, baseline training reproduced the trap
(ID $90.2\%$, OOD $45.9\%$, gap $44.3$~points). A plain fixed-weight
compositional loss showed no detectable difference from the full
$\sigma$-modulated curriculum (OOD $98.9\%$ vs.\ $98.9\%$,
$p = 0.99$), and the predicted phase-2
inflection appeared in every compositional-loss condition. The measured
schema-coherence proxy did not precede OOD improvement (partial
correlation $\approx 0$).

\textbf{Conclusion:} The $\sigma$-trap is a reproducible behavioural
pattern that a two-variable dynamical model describes. The claim level
is descriptive; whether SGD instantiates the posited dynamics remains an
open conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
% ============================================================

The standard framing of compositional generalisation failure is
precise and reproducible. Agents trained to high in-distribution
accuracy on sequence and language tasks fail near-completely when the
test distribution requires zero-shot recombination of primitives
trained in isolation. \cite{lake2018generalizationwithoutsystematicity}
demonstrated this on SCAN: seq2seq models achieve above 99\% accuracy
on random splits and below 2\% on the add-primitive split. The broader
benchmark literature is consistent --- COGS
\citep{kim2020cogscompositionalgeneralizationchallenge} reports
96--99\% in-distribution accuracy alongside 16--35\% compositional
generalisation accuracy, and PCFG-SET
\citep{hupkes2020compositionalitydecomposed} documents failures on
productivity, systematicity, and substitutivity tests across RNNs,
CNNs, and Transformers. High in-distribution accuracy can therefore
coexist with near-complete failure on compositional recombinations.

Why does this dissociation repeat across benchmarks? We formalise an
answer in terms of two distinct quantities. Standard training
pipelines have one formal target --- minimise expected loss over the
training distribution --- which efficiently grows what we call
\textbf{parametric depth} $\delta_A(d,t)$: the degree to which the
parameters have specialised to the training distribution. Nothing in
this objective formally targets a second, independent quantity:
\textbf{schema coherence} $\sigma_A(d,t)$, the degree to which
representations are organised around the compositional structure of
the task rather than surface statistics. When training optimises
$\delta_A$ without $\sigma_A$, agents can enter a stable low-coherence
state in which in-distribution performance is high but compositional
competence is not acquired. We call this stable state the
\textbf{$\sigma$-trap}. The mechanism is one of \emph{non-formation}
rather than active destruction: coherence simply fails to accumulate,
because the low-coherence state is locally stable --- the title's
``suppression'' denotes this stalling dynamic.

The two-variable dynamical model developed in Section~\ref{sec:core-framework}
is \emph{phenomenological}: the ordinary differential equations are
posited as a description of the observed training behaviour, not
derived from first principles of gradient descent. The connection
between real SGD trajectories and the model is stated explicitly as an
unproven Conjecture~\ref{conj:sgd-ode}. Under this model, the trap is a
locally stable low-$\sigma_A$ equilibrium; escape requires crossing a
critical threshold $\sigma_{\text{critical}}$, which corresponds to a
transcritical bifurcation of the $\sigma_A$ dynamics at $R_0 = 1$
(Proposition~4).

\paragraph*{Research question.}
Under standard training on compositional benchmarks, high-ID/low-OOD
agents arise systematically. Can a two-variable dynamical model of
schema coherence describe this behaviour, and do its predictions
survive a discriminating experiment --- in particular, a comparison
against a plain compositional-loss baseline that does not use the
model at all?

\paragraph*{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{A dynamical model of the $\sigma$-trap.}
  A two-variable ODE system (parametric depth $\delta_A$ and schema
  coherence $\sigma_A$) with a derived critical threshold
  $\sigma_{\text{critical}}$ and a transcritical bifurcation at
  $R_0 = 1$ (Section~\ref{sec:core-framework}). The model is
  phenomenological; the SGD--ODE connection is an explicit conjecture.
\item \textbf{A mechanism-gate experiment.}
  A four-arm experiment on the H-Bar compositional benchmark ($n = 15$
  per arm, 2000 steps) reproduces the trap --- baseline in-distribution
  accuracy $90.2\%$, out-of-distribution $45.9\%$, a gap of $44.3$
  points --- and shows that a \textbf{fixed-weight compositional-loss
  baseline shows no detectable difference from the full
  $\sigma$-modulated curriculum} (out-of-distribution $98.9\%$ vs.\
  $98.9\%$; Welch $p = 0.99$; Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}).
  The $\sigma$-scheduling is not necessary for the model's descriptive
  value.
\item \textbf{A negative leading-indicator result and a constructive
  diagnostic.}
  The measured schema-coherence proxy does \emph{not} precede OOD
  improvement (partial correlation $\approx 0$), while its
  representational-geometry component tracks compositional-loss
  exposure in every exposed arm --- including the arm with no $\sigma$
  dynamics (Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}). The first result
  constrains how the model may be interpreted; the second identifies
  the measurement component worth developing further.
\end{enumerate}

The extended framework --- the full Stage-1 proxy architecture, the
multi-domain extensions, and the phase structure beyond Phase~2 --- is
provided as complementary material in the companion technical report.
The present paper is self-contained and does not rely on it.

\section{Related Work and Gap Analysis}\label{sec:related-work}
% ============================================================

We review four literatures that bear on compositional generalisation
failure. To our knowledge, none models schema coherence as a dynamical
variable with its own evolution equation, suppression mechanism, and
critical threshold; making that gap precise is the purpose of this
review.

\subsection{Compositional Generalisation}\label{sec:compositional-generalisation}

The SCAN/COGS/CFQ/PCFG-SET benchmark family collectively documents the
high-ID/low-OOD failure mode empirically
\citep{mondorf2026compositionalarc}: models encode statistical
regularities rather than the compositional rules governing the
distribution \citep{lake2018generalizationwithoutsystematicity,
kim2020cogscompositionalgeneralizationchallenge,hupkes2020compositionalitydecomposed}.
Proposed remedies --- dual-process architectures
\citep{noviello2025neuroscienceinspiredualprocess}, structural
representability analyses \citep{bruns2025exploringcompositionalgeneralizationin},
mutual-exclusivity training \citep{jiang2022mutualexclusivity}, dataset
cartography \citep{swayamdipta2020datasetcartography}, and scaling
\citep{redhardt2025} --- improve in-distribution or lexical performance
without closing structural recombination gaps. These accounts
characterise the failure mode or intervene on it; to our knowledge,
none models the coherence of the learned representation as a state
variable whose dynamics determine when such interventions succeed.

\subsection{Grokking and Delayed Generalisation}\label{sec:grokking}

Grokking --- generalisation that appears only long after training loss
saturates --- is the temporal complement of the $\sigma$-trap
\citep{liu2022grokking}: the system lingers in a memorising regime and
transitions to generalisation late, or not at all. Microscopic accounts
attribute the transition to a lazy-to-rich feature-learning crossover
\citep{kumar2023grokkingeffectivetheory}, to a dimensional phase
transition in the gradient-field geometry
\citep{wang2026grokkingdimensionalphase}, to basin selection ranked by
the local learning coefficient
\citep{cullen2026basinselectiongrokking}, or to a threshold in the
Hessian spectrum for feature learning \citep{montanari2026}; loss-landscape
diagnostics can act as early-warning signals \citep{xu2026earlywarning}.
The $\sigma$-trap differs in two ways. First, it concerns a
\emph{stable} low-coherence equilibrium that standard training stays
in, rather than a generic escape event. Second, it is
compositional-specific: it predicts failure on recombination
conditional on matched in-distribution performance, not delayed
generalisation in general.

\subsection{Representation and Feature-Learning Dynamics}\label{sec:repr-dynamics}

Accounts that relate generalisation to the geometry of learned
representations include sharpness of the loss landscape
\citep{foret2021sharpnessawareminimization}, sparse-subnetwork
selection \citep{frankle2019lotteryticket}, and shortcut learning ---
reliance on surface features rather than structure
\citep{geirhos2020shortcutlearning}. These describe properties of the
solution or the strategy it encodes; the $\sigma$-trap describes the
representational state produced by the competition between shortcut
exploitation and schema formation, and derives a threshold
($\sigma_{\text{critical}}$) for when that competition is resolved in
favour of compositional competence.

\subsection{Curriculum Interventions}\label{sec:curriculum}

Easy-to-hard curricula provide representational scaffolding
\citep{bengio2009curriculumlearning,kumar2010selfpacedlearning,
portelas2020automaticcurriculumlearningdeep,narvekar2020curriculumlearningreinforcementlearning},
with recent evidence that ordering alone yields no robust advantage on
deductive-reasoning benchmarks \citep{mordig2026rethinkingeasytohard};
meta-learning over procedurally generated tasks can induce task-specific
schemas that transfer poorly across domains
\citep{mondorf2026compositionalarc}. The $\sigma$-modulated curriculum
studied in this paper is not a new ordering method: it couples the
training objective to the model's own coherence state. The gate
experiment isolates the value of that coupling by comparing it against
a fixed-weight compositional-loss baseline that uses no coupling at all
(Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}).

\paragraph{Positioning.}
Existing accounts of compositional generalisation have characterised
the problem through task structure, representation geometry, shortcut
learning, scaling, curriculum design, or optimisation dynamics. We
introduce a complementary dynamical construct --- schema coherence ---
that explicitly represents the degree to which learned representations
support systematic recombination. The claim is not that compositional
generalisation involves a training transition, but that this transition
can be described by a low-coherence attractor with a critical threshold.
The distinguishing empirical signature is compositional-specific failure
conditional on matched in-distribution accuracy. Following the
competing-variable design of Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}, a
schema-coherence measure must predict compositional OOD behaviour beyond
in-distribution accuracy, training loss, parameter norm, and
loss-landscape geometry; the gate experiment reports the first such
checks --- a fixed-weight control and partial-correlation controls.

\section{The $\sigma$-Trap: A Two-Variable Dynamical Model}\label{sec:core-framework}
% ============================================================

We model the training dynamics of an agent on a compositional task with
two state variables: \textbf{parametric depth} $\delta_A(d,t)$ and
\textbf{schema coherence} $\sigma_A(d,t)$. The model is deliberately
\emph{phenomenological}: the equations below are posited as a compact
description of the behaviour observed in the gate experiment
(Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}), not derived from first
principles of gradient-based optimisation. The link between real SGD
trajectories and the model is an explicit, unproven assumption:

\begin{conjecture}[SGD--ODE mapping; modelling assumption]
\label{conj:sgd-ode}
For the architecture and training protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}, there exist empirical
diagnostics --- parametric depth $\delta_A$ and schema coherence
$\sigma_A$ measured by the Stage-1 proxies (see the companion technical
report) --- whose evolution along SGD trajectories is well approximated,
in the scaling regime of the gate experiments, by the two-variable ODE
system (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:depth-ode} and \eqref{eq:sigma-ode}). The
conjecture licenses transferring the qualitative predictions of the ODE
analysis --- existence of a stable low-$\sigma_A$ equilibrium, the
$\sigma_{\text{critical}}$ crossing, and the Phase~2 inflection --- to
trained networks.
\end{conjecture}

\noindent\emph{Status.} Conjecture~1 is an assumption, not a result.
Nothing in this paper asserts that SGD itself instantiates the ODEs; the
ODEs are a phenomenological description. The mathematical results of
this section (Lemmas~1--2, Propositions~2--4) are theorems \emph{about
the ODE model} and remain valid whether or not the conjecture holds:
they are model-conditional, and no downstream result silently depends on
the conjecture being true. Empirical support is deferred to the gate
experiment (Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}), which detects the
Phase~2 entry signature in the $\sigma$-modulated arms; the conjecture
fails if the Stage-1 proxies fail \emph{simultaneously} --- i.e., if no
measured quantity tracks the modelled state, the ODE analysis has no
empirical counterpart. The gate results support the \emph{descriptive}
use of the model (the trap behaviour is reproduced) but provide no
evidence that measured $\sigma_A$ precedes OOD improvement (partial
correlation $\approx 0$; Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}). No
leading-indicator role may be attached to this conjecture.

\noindent In the two-variable core, $\alpha_A$ (attentional fidelity),
$\Omega_{SL}$ (shortcut-learning pressure), and $P_A$ (availability of
principled structure) enter as \emph{fixed or environmental inputs} ---
parameters of the core system, not state variables; their own dynamics
are treated in the companion. This keeps the state space strictly
two-dimensional, $\mathcal{X} = [0, \Delta_{\max}] \times [0, 1]$.

\subsection{State Variables}\label{sec:state-variables}

\subsubsection{Parametric Depth \texorpdfstring{$\delta_A(d,t)$}{delta\_A(d,t)}}\label{sec:depth}

The structural complexity of the agent's internal representation of the
task domain $d$: the degree to which parameters have specialised to the
training distribution. Bounded above by the domain frontier
$\Delta(d,t)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:depth-bounds}
0 \leq \delta_A(d,t) \leq \Delta(d,t)
\end{equation}
\textbf{Relative depth:}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-depth}
\delta_A^{\text{relative}}(d,t) = \frac{\delta_A(d,t)}{\Delta(d,t)} \in [0,1]
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Schema Coherence \texorpdfstring{$\sigma_A(d,t)$}{sigma\_A(d,t)}}\label{sec:schema-coherence}

Schema coherence $\sigma_A(d,t) \in [0,1]$ quantifies the degree to
which the agent's representation has been restructured around the
compositional structure of the task --- the operators and recombination
rules that generate the distribution --- rather than surface statistics.
High $\sigma_A$ indicates that the representation encodes compositional
operators applicable beyond the training distribution; low $\sigma_A$
indicates memorisation of surface patterns that fail to transfer.

$\sigma_A$ is distinct from OOD accuracy in kind: it is a property of
the \emph{representation} (internal state), while OOD accuracy is a
property of \emph{behavioural output}; two agents can achieve identical
OOD accuracy through different mechanisms (memorisation vs.\ genuine
recombination), and only the latter exhibits high $\sigma_A$.
Operationally, $\sigma_A$ is measured at training time by the Stage-1
proxies --- gradient-composition alignment (GCA), representational-
geometry alignment (RGA), and augmentation consistency (AC) --- fused as
$\tilde{\sigma}_A$. Concretely, GCA computes the cosine alignment
between per-step parameter gradients and a compositional reference
direction derived from the task's recombination rules; RGA scores the
similarity (CKA-style) between the representational geometry of
training inputs and of their compositional recombinations; and AC
measures prediction consistency under primitive-substitution
augmentation. The three signals are normalised to $[0,1]$ and combined
as a weighted average; the gate experiment used the two-signal GCA+RGA
sub-fusion, and the full three-signal protocol, its two-stage
calibration, and the estimation bounds are given in the companion
technical report --- of which the sketch above is a brief summary.
Throughout, $\sigma_A$ denotes the theoretical construct, $\tilde{\sigma}_A$
its training-time estimate, and $\hat{\sigma}_A$ the post-hoc diagnostic;
the construct is not directly observable, and the estimates are
imperfect (Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}). The Stage-2 diagnostic
$\hat{\sigma}_A = \text{Acc}_{\text{OOD}}/\text{Acc}_{\text{ID}}$ is
defined from the same accuracies it describes and is used in this paper
\emph{post-hoc} only, as a descriptive summary of the measured gap ---
never as a training signal and never as evidence for a causal
relationship (Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}).

\subsection{Mastery and Decay}\label{sec:mastery-set}

The \textbf{mastery set} collects the domains in which the agent has
both sufficient depth and sufficient schema coherence:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mastery-set}
M_A(t) = \{d \in D : \delta_A(d,t) > \theta_{\delta} \cdot \Delta(d,t) \text{ AND } \sigma_A(d,t) > \theta_{\sigma}\}
\end{equation}
with $\theta_{\delta}, \theta_{\sigma} \in [0,1]$ the depth and
coherence mastery thresholds (Appendix~\ref{app:notation}).

Learned structure decays, and schema coherence protects against decay:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-decay}
\lambda_c^{\text{eff}}(d,t) = \lambda_c \cdot (1 - \gamma_\sigma \cdot \sigma_A(d,t))
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:depth-decay}
\dot{\delta}_A(d,t)\big|_{\text{decay}} = -\lambda_c \cdot (1 - \gamma_\sigma \cdot \sigma_A(d,t)) \cdot \delta_A(d,t) \cdot (1 - r_A(d,t))
\end{equation}
where $r_A(d,t)$ is a retrieval-practice indicator. The multiplicative
form keeps $\lambda_c^{\text{eff}} \in [0, \lambda_c]$ for all
$\sigma_A \in [0,1]$ and $\gamma_\sigma \in [0,1]$: coherence can only
reduce decay, never reverse it.

\subsection{Growth Dynamics}\label{sec:growth}

\subsubsection{Depth Growth ODE}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:depth-ode}
\dot{\delta}_A(d,t) = f_{\text{learn}}(d,t) \cdot \eta(d,t) \cdot T_A(d,t) - \lambda_c \cdot (1 - \gamma_\sigma \cdot \sigma_A(d,t)) \cdot \delta_A(d,t) \cdot (1 - r_A(d,t))
\end{equation}
with learning efficiency in Gompertz form:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gompertz}
\eta(d,t) = \eta_{\text{max}} \cdot \exp(-a \cdot \exp(-b \cdot \delta_A^{\text{relative}}(d,t)))
\end{equation}
where $\eta_{\max}$ is the maximal learning efficiency and
$a, b > 0$ shape the Gompertz curve (Appendix~\ref{app:notation}).
Depth grows when learning efficiency and time-on-task are large, and
decays when schema coherence is low (little protection from forgetting).

\subsubsection{Schema Coherence ODE}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma-ode}
\dot{\sigma}_A(d,t) = \sigma_A(d,t) \cdot \bigl[ \rho \cdot P_A(d,t) \cdot \alpha_A \cdot (1 - \gamma_{\sigma} \cdot \sigma_A(d,t)) - \epsilon_{\sigma} \cdot \Omega_{SL}(d,t) \bigr]
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is the schema growth rate, $P_A$ the availability of
principled structure, $\alpha_A$ the attentional fidelity (a fixed
parameter of the two-variable core; its full dynamics are in the
companion), $\gamma_\sigma$ the saturation coefficient, $\epsilon_\sigma$
the shortcut-suppression coefficient, and $\Omega_{SL}$ the
shortcut-learning pressure.

\noindent\textit{Intuition.} The $\sigma_A$ ODE has an autocatalytic
structure: growth is proportional to current coherence ($\sigma_A$)
times the net driving force in the bracket. The bracket captures whether
principled understanding builds faster than shortcut pressure erodes it.
When $\sigma_A$ is near zero, growth stalls entirely because the
$\sigma_A$ factor suppresses the whole right-hand side. This creates the
critical bifurcation at the heart of the model: below a threshold, the
$\sigma_A = 0$ state is stable and self-perpetuating --- the
$\sigma$-trap; above it, coherence grows toward a positive equilibrium.

\subsection{Mathematical Foundations}\label{sec:math-foundations}

The coupled system (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:depth-ode} and \eqref{eq:sigma-ode})
is a two-dimensional nonlinear ODE on the state
$\mathbf{x}_A(t) = (\delta_A(t), \sigma_A(t)) \in \mathcal{X}$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:state-vector}
\mathbf{x}_A(t) = (\delta_A(t), \sigma_A(t))^{\mathsf{T}},
\qquad
\mathcal{X} = [0, \Delta_{\max}] \times [0, 1]
\end{equation}
(the full state vector with breadth, attention, and metacognitive
variables is treated in the companion). We suppress the domain index $d$
throughout this section.

\textbf{Lemma 1 (Local Existence and Uniqueness).} % relabelled from Prop 3.1
\emph{For any initial condition $\mathbf{x}_A(0) \in \text{int}(\mathcal{X})$,
the system admits a unique solution $\mathbf{x}_A(t)$ on a maximal
interval $[0, T_{\max})$ with $T_{\max} > 0$.}

\emph{Proof.} In Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\textbf{Lemma 2 (Forward Invariance and Boundedness).}
% relabelled from Prop 3.2
\emph{$\mathcal{X}$ is forward-invariant under the clamped dynamics
(the upper bound $\sigma_A = 1$ is enforced by the solver's boundary
clipping; see the proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}): if
$\mathbf{x}_A(0) \in \mathcal{X}$, then $\mathbf{x}_A(t) \in \mathcal{X}$
for all $t \in [0, T_{\max})$. If $\Delta_{\max} < \infty$, then
$T_{\max} = \infty$ (global existence).}

\emph{Proof.} In Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\textbf{Proposition 2 (Equilibria of the Two-Variable Core).}
% relabelled from Prop 3.4
\emph{For constant frontier $\Delta$ and fixed parameters, the system
admits exactly three equilibrium classes:}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Trivial equilibrium} $E_0 = (0, 0)$: no knowledge
  acquired. Exists iff the effective training input vanishes
  ($f_{\text{learn}} \cdot T_A = 0$): under the Gompertz form
  $\eta(0) = \eta_{\max} e^{-a} > 0$, so $\dot{\delta}_A > 0$ at
  $\delta_A = 0$ whenever training input is nonzero (consistent with
  Lemma~2's boundary check).
\item \textbf{Surface-learning equilibrium}
  $E_S = (\delta_S^*, 0)$: depth acquired but $\sigma_A = 0$. Exists
  when shortcut-learning pressure is high ($\epsilon_\sigma \Omega_{SL} >
  \rho P_A \alpha_A$). This is the $\sigma$-trap.
\item \textbf{Schema-coherent equilibrium}
  $E_C = (\delta_C^*, \sigma_C^*)$ with $\sigma_C^* > 0$: both depth and
  coherence positive; exists and is stable iff $R_0 > 1$, i.e.\ iff the
  \emph{fixed} parameter $\alpha_A > \epsilon_\sigma \Omega_{SL} /
  (\rho P_A)$ (a condition on the parameter, not on its dynamics).
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} In Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\textbf{Proposition 3 (Low-$\sigma$ Trap: Global Stability).}
% relabelled from Thm 4.1; model-conditional per the P05 relabel plan
\emph{If $R_0 < 1$ --- i.e.\ the shortcut pressure dominates,
$\epsilon_\sigma \Omega_{SL} > \rho P_A \alpha_A$ --- then, under the
dynamics of \eqref{eq:sigma-ode} alone, $\dot{\sigma}_A < 0$ for all
$\sigma_A \in (0,1]$, and the schema-free state $\sigma_A = 0$ is
\emph{globally} attracting on $[0,1]$: the model's low-coherence state
is a stable trap. This is a statement about the model, not about real
SGD; whether SGD enters this regime is the subject of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:sgd-ode}.}

\emph{Proof.} In Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\textbf{Proposition 4 ($\sigma_{\text{critical}}$ and the Transcritical
Bifurcation).}
% relabelled from Prop 4.1; main theorem of the narrow paper
\emph{Define the basic schema growth ratio
\begin{equation}
R_0 = \frac{\rho \cdot P_A \cdot \alpha_A}{\epsilon_\sigma \cdot \Omega_{SL}}.
\end{equation}
The Phase~1$\to$2 transition threshold is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma-critical}
\sigma_{\text{critical}} = \frac{1}{\gamma_\sigma} \cdot \left(1 - R_0^{-1}\right).
\end{equation}
When $R_0 > 1$, the schema-free equilibrium $\sigma_A = 0$ becomes
unstable and the schema-coherent equilibrium emerges via a
\emph{transcritical} bifurcation at $R_0 = 1$.}

\emph{Proof.} In Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\noindent\emph{Intuition.} The parameter $R_0$ is borrowed from
epidemiology: when $R_0 > 1$, one unit of schema coherence generates more
than one unit of additional coherence --- understanding snowballs. When
$R_0 < 1$, schemas die out and the agent remains in the trap. Standard
training keeps $R_0 < 1$ by inflating shortcut-learning pressure
$\Omega_{SL}$ (Proposition~4); interventions
that raise $R_0$ above $1$ --- in the gate experiment, exposure to
compositional loss --- make the coherent equilibrium stable.

\noindent\emph{Against a purely logistic reading.} On a single OOD
trajectory the model's observable signature can resemble an S-shaped
logistic growth curve, so one may ask why a two-variable ODE is needed
rather than a three-parameter logistic fit. The difference is
structural: a logistic curve describes monotonic growth from any
initial state, whereas the model's content is the phase-space geometry
--- a \emph{stable} low-coherence equilibrium $E_S$ (the trap, with
$\dot{\sigma}_A < 0$ when $R_0 < 1$), a coherent attractor $E_C$, and
an exchange of stability at $R_0 = 1$ (Proposition~4). A fitted sigmoid
cannot represent the persistent stability of the failure state, nor the
parameter condition under which that state loses stability; the model's
predictions concern that geometry rather than the shape of any single
curve. Whether the gate trajectories formally discriminate the model
from logistic/Gompertz alternatives is an open question
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\noindent\emph{Bifurcation parameter and moving threshold.} The
bifurcation parameter is $R_0$ crossing $1$; $\sigma_{\text{critical}}$
is the value of the emerging positive branch at the bifurcation. Since
$\Omega_{SL}(d,t)$ and $P_A(d,t)$ are time-dependent, so is
$\sigma_{\text{critical}}(t)$: Phase~2 entry ($\sigma_A >
\sigma_{\text{critical}}$) is a crossing enabled by a decline in
shortcut-learning pressure, which resolves the apparent paradox that
$\sigma_A$ cannot exceed its own stable equilibrium. Section~7 builds
the phase trigger on this moving threshold.
Figure~\ref{fig:bifurcation} illustrates the bifurcation structure and
the $(\delta_A, \sigma_A)$ phase portrait with the three equilibria.

\section{Phase Structure: The Phase~1 to Phase~2 Transition}\label{sec:phase-structure}
% ============================================================

The training arc relevant to the narrow model comprises three phases
(P0--P2), indexed by the two-variable state
($\delta_A^{\text{relative}}, \sigma_A$). The phases beyond Phase~2 ---
intersection discovery, multi-domain navigation --- depend on the
multi-domain machinery $\Psi_A$ and are treated in the companion. The
phases are defined by the bifurcation structure of
Section~\ref{sec:core-framework}, not by retrospective loss-curve
observation: each phase boundary is a qualitative threshold of the
$\sigma_A$ dynamics (Figure~\ref{fig:phases}).

\subsection*{Phase~0 --- Pre-Domain Initialisation}

$\delta_A$ near-zero across all domains; no structured training
dynamics.

\subsection*{Phase~1 --- Asymmetric Initialisation}

First sustained depth investment in candidate domains; $\delta_A$
growing, $\sigma_A \approx 0$. Under the model, this is the regime in
which the $\sigma$-trap forms: with $R_0 < 1$ the low-coherence state
is globally attracting (Proposition~4).

\subsection*{Phase~2 --- Depth Acceleration and Schema Crystallisation}

\textbf{Trigger:} $\sigma_A(d,t) > \sigma_{\text{critical}}(t)$ for at
least one mastery candidate, where
$\sigma_{\text{critical}}(t) = \gamma_\sigma^{-1}\bigl(1 - R_0(t)^{-1}\bigr)$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma-critical}) is the \emph{moving} threshold of
Section~\ref{sec:core-framework}. The trigger is the transcritical
bifurcation at $R_0 = 1$ (Proposition~4): when
shortcut-learning pressure declines enough to push $R_0$ above $1$, the
schema-free equilibrium loses stability and coherence grows toward
$\sigma_C^* = \min\bigl(\sigma_{\text{critical}}, 1\bigr)$. Phase~2
entry produces a characteristic acceleration pattern in the OOD
performance trajectory.

\subsection{Bifurcation Relation and the Empirical Inflection Signature}\label{sec:bifurcation}

The Phase~1$\to$2 transition is not an arbitrary threshold: it is the
transcritical bifurcation of Proposition~4. The phase boundary is the
parameter condition $R_0 = 1$; $\sigma_{\text{critical}}$ is the value
of the emerging branch at the boundary (Section~\ref{sec:core-framework}).
Because $\Omega_{SL}(d,t)$ and $P_A(d,t)$ are time-dependent, the
threshold moves, and Phase~2 entry ($\sigma_A > \sigma_{\text{critical}}$)
is a crossing enabled by a decline in shortcut-learning pressure.

\noindent\emph{Empirically testable signature (Prediction~9,
Section~\ref{sec:predictions}).} Under $\sigma$-modulated training,
Phase~2 entry is detectable as an inflection --- a change-point --- in
the OOD accuracy trajectory: the characteristic acceleration pattern.
The gate experiment observes this signature: in the $\sigma$-modulated
arms, OOD accuracy accelerates between steps 200 and 400 (from
$\approx 42\%$ to $\approx 99\%$), while the baseline arm shows no such
inflection (Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}). Because measured
$\sigma_A$ does not precede OOD improvement
(Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}), the inflection is reported as
a descriptive signature of the model's phase structure, not as evidence
of a leading indicator.

\noindent\emph{Model vs.\ SGD.} The phase structure is a property of
the model. Whether SGD trajectories enter the described phases is the
subject of Conjecture~\ref{conj:sgd-ode}; the gate provides the
empirical signatures --- the baseline arm stays in the trap, the
$\sigma$-modulated arms enter Phase~2 --- without asserting that the
scheduling is necessary for the improvement
(Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}).

% ============================================================
\section{Predictions}\label{sec:predictions}
% ============================================================

The narrow model makes a single flagship prediction. The extended
prediction set --- predictions~1--8, hypotheses 6.1--6.3, and
meta-prediction~M1 --- depends on variables removed from the narrow
model ($\Psi_A$, $\beta_A$, $D^*$, the multi-domain machinery) and is
stated and analysed in the companion.

\subsection{Prediction 9 --- Phase~2 Entry Inflection}

The transition from Phase~1 to Phase~2 produces a measurable inflection
in the OOD accuracy trajectory. Before the crossing of the moving
threshold $\sigma_{\text{critical}}(t)$
(Section~\ref{sec:phase-structure}), OOD accuracy grows slowly; after
the crossing, the trajectory accelerates markedly --- the
characteristic Phase~2 acceleration pattern.

\noindent\emph{Operationalisation.} The prediction is tested by
segmented (piecewise-linear) regression on the per-run OOD accuracy
trajectory, with the breakpoint $\hat{\tau}$ estimated from the data:
\[
\text{OOD}(t) = \beta_0 + \beta_1\, t + \beta_2\, (t - \tau)_+ + e_t,
\qquad (x)_+ = \max(0, x),
\]
where $\tau$ is the Phase~2 entry time and a significant positive
$\beta_2$ constitutes the inflection (pre-registered: detectable slope
change of $\Delta\beta = 0.02$ accuracy units per timestep; in the gate,
evaluation every 25 steps over 2000 steps, i.e.\ 80 trajectory points
per run).

\noindent\emph{Status in the gate experiment.} The gate observes the
predicted signature: in the $\sigma$-modulated arms, OOD accuracy
accelerates from $\approx 42\%$ to $\approx 99\%$ between steps 200 and
400, while the baseline arm shows no such inflection
(Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}). Two qualifications follow
from the gate verdict. First, the inflection is a \emph{descriptive}
signature of the model's phase structure: because measured $\sigma_A$
does not precede OOD improvement (partial correlation $\approx 0$;
Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}), the prediction does not license
a leading-indicator or causal reading. Second, the fixed-weight arm
achieves the same OOD outcome as the $\sigma$-modulated arms, so the
prediction characterises the behaviour of models exposed to
compositional loss, not the necessity of the $\sigma$-scheduling itself
(Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}).

% ============================================================
\section{Empirical Grounding}\label{sec:empirical}
% ============================================================

\paragraph*{Proof-of-concept scope.}
The experiments in this section test whether the qualitative
predictions of the model --- the existence of a stable low-coherence
state under standard training, the closing of the OOD gap under
compositional-loss exposure, and the Phase~2 inflection --- are
observable in a controlled setting. They do \emph{not} validate
generality: all results are on a single synthetic benchmark, one
architecture, and $n = 15$ seeds per condition. Claims about other
benchmarks, architectures, or scales remain open
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph*{Benchmark.}
The H-Bar benchmark is a SCAN-style compositional sequence-to-sequence
task: commands combine primitives (jump/walk/run/look), modifiers
(twice/thrice), and combinators (and/after/around/opposite), and the
target is the token-wise composition. In-distribution (ID) test items
are short combinations within the training grammar; out-of-distribution
(OOD) items are novel recombinations never seen in training (mean
length 3.3 tokens vs.\ 1.6 in training). Vocabulary size is 27; the
training set has 16{,}000 examples, the ID test 4{,}000, and the OOD
test 1{,}500. Two notes on benchmark choice and provenance. H-Bar is
deliberately SCAN-like but smaller: its grammar enumerates all
primitive/modifier/combinator recombinations, so the OOD split is
compositional by construction (novel combinations of trained primitives)
rather than merely distributional, and compositional-loss exposure can
be defined cleanly over the recombination space. The benchmark is
generated programmatically from the grammar specification in the gate
configuration; the generator, splits, and analysis scripts are committed
with the code and the raw per-seed tables are in the companion
(Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}).

\paragraph*{Design.}
Four conditions, $n = 15$ runs each (2000 steps; seed discipline
$s_r = r \cdot 42 + 7$, deterministic cuDNN flags): \emph{baseline}
(plain task loss), \emph{fixed-weight} (constant-weight compositional
loss every 5 steps, no $\sigma$ modulation --- a deliberately strong
plain-compositional baseline), and the two $\sigma$-modulated curricula
\emph{additive} and \emph{multiplicative}
(Section~\ref{sec:core-framework}). The measured Stage-1 proxy
$\tilde{\sigma}_A$ (GCA+RGA fusion; companion) is recorded every 25
steps; the full experimental protocol and hyperparameters are in
Section~\ref{sec:empirical-validation}.

\paragraph*{Power.}
Effect sizes are reported per comparison; no pooled aggregate
$d$ is used. For the primary comparison --- baseline vs.\ additive OOD
accuracy --- the observed effect is $d \approx 14.0$
(pooled $\sigma \approx 3.8$~pp), giving $> 99.9\%$ power at
$\alpha = 0.05$ for $n = 15$; the per-comparison $t$, $p$, and effect
sizes appear in Table~\ref{tab:gate-welch}. The $N = 500$
pre-registered protocol (companion) remains a community replication
resource rather than a requirement for the primary claims.

\paragraph*{Setup and transparency.}
Architecture: 2-layer, 4-head Transformer ($d = 128$, $\dim_{\text{ff}} = 512$,

\paragraph*{Compositional-loss exposure closes the gap; the
$\sigma$-scheduling adds nothing.}
All three compositional-loss conditions far exceed baseline OOD
(Table~\ref{tab:gate-welch}; $d \geq 6.7$, all $p < 10^{-14}$). The

-0.01$~pp, Welch $p = 0.99$, $d = -0.00$ --- a plain constant-weight

pre-registered). Multiplicative is marginally (non-significantly) lower
($p = 0.059$ vs.\ fixed-weight; $p = 0.053$ vs.\ additive). Two
consequences follow, consistent with the gate verdict: (i)~the
$\sigma$-scheduling is not necessary for the OOD outcome --- exposure
to compositional loss is what matters; (ii)~the model's empirical value
is \emph{descriptive}: it characterises the trajectory (stable trap in
baseline, phase-2 acceleration under comp exposure) rather than
explaining why the scheduled intervention wins, because it does not.
Within the model, the mechanism is a drop in shortcut-learning pressure:
exposure to compositional loss makes surface statistics less predictive
of the target, lowering $\Omega_{SL}$ and pushing $R_0$ above $1$
(Proposition~4). The $\sigma$-scheduling therefore served as a
\emph{causal probe} --- it tests whether dynamic coupling between the
loss and the coherence state is necessary to escape the trap --- and
the answer is no: any sufficient pressure on compositional structure
triggers the same Phase~2 transition.

\paragraph*{Does measured $\tilde{\sigma}_A$ precede OOD? No.}
The measured Stage-1 proxy (GCA+RGA fusion) is recorded every 25 steps.
The dynamic leading test --- partial correlation
$\tilde{\sigma}_{A,t} \to \text{OOD}_{t+1}$ controlling OOD$_t$,
training loss, and parameter norm --- gives pooled ODE-guided mean
$+0.018$ (one-sided $t$, $p = 0.24$): no predictive relationship.
The crossing-time test is degenerate: $\tilde{\sigma}_A$ starts at its
normalised maximum (GCA $\approx 0.95$ on the random-initialised model;
Figure~\ref{fig:gate-sigma}), so the crossing time is zero by
construction. The high initial value is expected rather than
informative: at random initialisation the gradient is dominated by the
batch's shared input statistics, so its alignment with any fixed
reference direction is trivially high, and the alignment then decays as
parameters specialise to the training distribution. The proxy's
\emph{level} therefore cannot be read as achieved schema coherence;
only its \emph{trend} is informative --- which is why the
partial-correlation test, not the crossing-time test, is decisive here.
The scheduled knob reproduces the archived finding
(leads-fraction $0.00$ in all arms). These results constrain the model
to its descriptive role (Section~\ref{sec:predictions}).

\paragraph*{Competing-variable check.}
Final OOD regressed on $[\tilde{\sigma}_{A,\text{final}}$, final loss,
parameter norm, ID accuracy] (standardised OLS, per arm): the
$\tilde{\sigma}_A$ coefficient is small or negative everywhere
(baseline $-0.35$, fixed-weight $+0.04$, additive $-0.12$,
multiplicative $-0.06$), and the Spearman correlation
$\rho(\tilde{\sigma}_A, \text{OOD})$ is $+0.13$ or lower except in
baseline, where it is \emph{negative} ($-0.62$, $p = 0.013$). The
fused proxy adds no positive predictive value beyond loss/norm/ID ---
the ``not just $\tilde{\sigma}_A$'' defence the other direction. The
RGA component alone does track OOD improvement (partial correlation
$+0.125$, $p \approx 0.005$, pooled ODE arms) --- but equally in the
fixed-weight arm, which has no $\sigma$ dynamics: it tracks
compositional-loss exposure, not the $\sigma$-scheduling
(consequence-of-exposure interpretation).

\paragraph*{Proxy status.}
Taken together, these results show that the fused Stage-1 proxy is
\emph{not a valid leading indicator in its current form}: its level is
dominated by the initialisation artefact (GCA $\approx 0.95$ at
random init), and its trend does not precede OOD improvement. This is a
measurement limitation, not evidence about the underlying construct ---
$\sigma_A$ may change before OOD improvement while the proxy fails to
detect it (a sensitivity failure, not a falsification of the model). The
one component that does track the transition, RGA, does so equally in
the arm with no $\sigma$ dynamics, which identifies it as an
\emph{exposure tracker} (it reports that compositional loss is being
applied) rather than a coherence meter. Developing a non-circular,
training-time proxy that reliably tracks $\sigma_A$ prior to OOD takeoff
remains an open instrumentation challenge (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph*{Circularity.}
As everywhere in this paper, the Stage-2 diagnostic is used
\emph{post-hoc} only --- never as a training signal or as evidence for
a causal relationship; the full statement is in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}. All training-time statements use the
Stage-1 proxy $\tilde{\sigma}_A$ (companion).

\subsection{Open mathematical questions}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Global existence with a time-dependent frontier.}
  Lemma~2's invariance argument assumes a constant frontier $\Delta$;
  the moving-boundary condition $\dot{\delta}_A \leq \dot{\Delta}$ at
  $\delta_A = \Delta(t)$ has not been verified for the gate's parameter
  regime.
\item \textbf{Timescale bounds.}
  The IMEX-RK stability analysis (Appendix~\ref{app:stability}) treats
  the two-variable core; stiffness bounds as $\sigma_A \to 1$ remain
  open.
\item \textbf{Stability landscape beyond the core.}
  The equilibria of the extended system (breadth, attention,
  metacognition; companion) and their relation to the core bifurcation
  are unanalysed.
\item \textbf{Model selection.}
  Whether the gate's 80-point trajectories discriminate the two-variable
  ODE from simpler single-curve descriptions (logistic/Gompertz fits of
  the OOD trajectory) has not been tested formally; the model's
  phase-space content --- a stable trap and an exchange of stability at
  $R_0 = 1$ --- is not expressed by such fits, but the discrimination
  question itself remains open. Note that the $\sigma_A$ ODE is itself a
  logistic-growth equation in projection
  ($\dot{\sigma}_A = r_{\text{net}}\,\sigma_A - k\,\sigma_A^2$), so an
  AIC-style comparison of trajectory \emph{shape} against a fitted
  logistic is not a well-posed discrimination by construction; what
  remains open is latent-parameter identifiability and the phase-space
  interpretation, not the goodness of fit of any single curve.
\end{enumerate}

The adoption guide and the expanded failure-mode catalogue are in the
companion; they are outside the scope of this paper.

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.4pt}

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================

Standard training on a compositional benchmark reliably produces the
pattern this paper set out to describe: high in-distribution accuracy
alongside a persistent out-of-distribution gap ($44.3$~points in the
gate's baseline arm), which compositional-loss exposure closes --- with
or without the $\sigma$-modulated scheduling ($p = 0.99$). We model this
behaviour with a two-variable dynamical system --- parametric depth and
schema coherence --- whose qualitative predictions (a stable low-$\sigma$
state, a critical threshold, a Phase~2 inflection) are observable in the
experiment, and whose measured coherence proxy does not precede OOD
improvement. The observed Phase~2 signature is therefore a
\emph{descriptive} property of the model's phase structure, not
evidence that $\sigma_A$ is a leading causal variable. The model is a
\emph{dynamical description} of
the $\sigma$-trap, not a demonstrated mechanism; whether SGD itself
instantiates the ODEs is an explicit, open conjecture
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:sgd-ode}), and the research programme built on it
is labelled as such.

Three next steps follow. \textbf{(i) Pre-registration at scale:} run
the $N = 500$ protocol (companion) across the SCAN/COGS/PCFG-SET family
to test the architecture- and benchmark-generality rows of
Table~\ref{tab:claim-status}. \textbf{(ii) Direct proxy validation:}
test the Stage-1 proxy against the Stage-2 diagnostic and against
competing measures (sharpness, LLC-style probes) --- the open
``unique construct'' row. \textbf{(iii) The SGD--ODE connection:} seek
a derivation or a sharper falsification of Conjecture~1, which
determines whether the descriptive model can be upgraded to a
mechanistic one.

\paragraph*{Research programme.}
The foundational hypothesis underlying this line of work --- that
schema-coherence dynamics may be a general mechanism underlying
transitions from statistical competence to systematic understanding,
beyond the benchmark studied here --- is stated explicitly as a
\emph{labelled future hypothesis}, not a claim of this paper. It is the
programme that the three next steps above are designed to test; nothing
in the present results licenses it as a conclusion.

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.4pt}

% ============================================================
\section*{Broader Impact}\label{sec:broader-impact}
% ============================================================

The training curricula studied here couple the loss to a
schema-coherence state and improve compositional competence in a
controlled benchmark. The same mechanism is in principle reversible: a
curriculum that drives the coherence state below threshold could
suppress compositional competence while preserving in-distribution
performance --- a dual-use direction this paper does not develop. The
results are limited to one synthetic benchmark and one architecture, and
no claims about deployed systems are made.

\section{IMEX-RK Stability at Critical Equilibria}\label{app:stability}
% ============================================================

This appendix verifies that the ARS(2,3,2) IMEX Runge--Kutta tableau
is stable when evaluated at the
critical equilibria $E_S$ and $E_C$ from Proposition~2. The gate
experiment itself used the fixed-step Euler scheme of
Section~\ref{sec:numerical-protocol}, whose stability is ensured by
Lemma~2's boundedness; the analysis below therefore applies to the
ARS(2,3,2) scheme retained as the numerical-rigour defence.

The Jacobians below describe the slow subsystem of the extended model
(companion), with state order $(\hat{M}_A, \Xi_A^P, \Xi_A^I)$:
$\nu_M$ is the tracking gain of the agent's self-model $\hat{M}_A$ of
its own schema coherence (Extension~4 of the companion), $\xi_M$ the
shortcut-pressure erosion coefficient acting on that self-model,
$\kappa_P, \kappa_I$ the executive-control tracking rates of the
principled-structure and integration channels (Extension~3), and
$\kappa_{P,\Xi}, \kappa_{I,\Xi}$ the off-diagonal couplings of the
executive-control subsystem at the schema-coherent equilibrium
$E_C$.

\subsection{Jacobian at Equilibria}

For the two-variable core the stability analysis reduces to the scalar
linearisation of \eqref{eq:sigma-ode} (Proposition~4); the Jacobian of
the full extended system at $E_S$ is treated in the companion. As the
numerical-rigour defence for the core, the IMEX stability evaluation
applies the ARS(2,3,2) scheme at $E_S$ and $E_C$ with:
\begin{equation}
J_S = \left.\frac{\partial f_{\text{slow}}}{\partial x_{\text{slow}}}\right|_{E_S}
= \begin{pmatrix}
-\nu_M & 0 & 0 \\
0 & -\kappa_P & 0 \\
0 & 0 & -\kappa_I
\end{pmatrix} + O(\epsilon),
\end{equation}
where $O(\epsilon)$ captures the coupling back from the fast subsystem
$(\delta_A, \sigma_A, \alpha_A)$. At $E_C$, the Jacobian includes
the stabilising contribution from the schema-coherent equilibrium:
\begin{equation}
J_C = \begin{pmatrix}
-\nu_M & \xi_M \sigma_A^* & 0 \\
0 & -\kappa_P & \kappa_{P,\Xi} \\
0 & \kappa_{I,\Xi} & -\kappa_I
\end{pmatrix} + O(\epsilon),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_A^*$ is the $\sigma_A$ value at $E_C$.

\subsection{Eigenvalue Analysis}

The eigenvalues of $J_S$ are $-\nu_M$, $-\kappa_P$, $-\kappa_I$; all are
real and negative with magnitudes $\lambda_{\max} = \max(\nu_M, \kappa_P,
\kappa_I) \approx 0.1\text{--}1.0$ (dimensionless). The eigenvalues of
$J_C$ are shifted by the off-diagonal coupling but remain strictly
negative when $R_0 > 1$ (transcritical bifurcation condition).

\subsection{Stability Region Overlay}

The ARS(2,3,2) implicit tableau is $A$-stable: its stability region
contains the entire left half-plane $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) < 0$.
Since all eigenvalues of both $J_S$ and $J_C$ have strictly negative
real parts, the IMEX scheme is unconditionally stable for any step size
$\Delta t$ when the slow subsystem is integrated implicitly.

The explicit tableau for the fast subsystem is conditionally stable.
Its stability region is approximately a circle of radius $r \approx
2.5$ in the left half-plane. The fast eigenvalues (from the
$\delta_A$, $\sigma_A$, $\alpha_A$ subsystem) satisfy
$|\lambda_{\text{fast}}| \lesssim 1/\epsilon$ where $\epsilon$ is
the timescale separation parameter. For $\epsilon \lesssim 0.1$,
the condition $|\lambda_{\text{fast}}|\Delta t < 2.5$ is satisfied
by our adaptive step-size controller (relative tolerance $10^{-6}$).

\subsection{Verdict}

The IMEX scheme is stable at both $E_S$ and $E_C$, encompassing the
bifurcation point where stiffness is most problematic.
No special treatment beyond the ARS(2,3,2) tableau is required for
these critical equilibria.

\section{Proofs of the Core Results}\label{app:proofs}
% ============================================================

This appendix collects the proofs of the results stated in
Section~\ref{sec:core-framework}; statements are repeated for
self-containment. The results are theorems about the ODE model and
remain valid whether or not Conjecture~\ref{conj:sgd-ode} holds.

\paragraph*{Proof of Lemma 1 (local existence and uniqueness).}
The right-hand sides of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:depth-ode} and
\eqref{eq:sigma-ode} are polynomial in the state variables (products
$\sigma_A \cdot \delta_A$, $\sigma_A^2$) and piecewise-smooth (the
Gompertz term \eqref{eq:gompertz}), hence locally Lipschitz on
$\text{int}(\mathcal{X})$; by the Picard--Lindel\"of theorem a unique
local solution exists on a maximal interval $[0, T_{\max})$.
$\square$

\paragraph*{Proof of Lemma 2 (forward invariance and boundedness).}
At $\delta_A = 0$ the growth term dominates ($\dot{\delta}_A \geq 0$);
at $\delta_A = \Delta(d,t)$ the decay term dominates, assuming a
constant frontier $\Delta$ (as in Proposition~2) --- for time-dependent
frontiers, invariance of $\delta_A \leq \Delta(t)$ requires the
moving-boundary condition $\dot{\delta}_A \leq \dot{\Delta}$ at the
boundary. At $\sigma_A = 0$, the $\sigma_A$ factor makes
$\dot{\sigma}_A = 0$ (an invariant manifold, not a reflecting
boundary). At $\sigma_A = 1$, $\dot{\sigma}_A = \rho P_A \alpha_A
(1 - \gamma_\sigma) - \epsilon_\sigma \Omega_{SL}$ is \emph{not}
inherently $\leq 0$; the upper bound is enforced by clamping in the
phenomenological solver, so the invariance statement applies to the
clamped dynamics. Since $\mathcal{X}$ is compact when
$\Delta_{\max} < \infty$, solutions cannot escape to infinity, so
$T_{\max} = \infty$. $\square$

\paragraph*{Proof of Proposition 2 (equilibria of the two-variable
core).}
Setting $\dot{\sigma}_A = 0$ in \eqref{eq:sigma-ode}: the
$\sigma_A = 0$ branch is always a fixed point; the interior fixed point
solves $\rho P_A \alpha_A (1 - \gamma_\sigma \sigma_A^*) =
\epsilon_\sigma \Omega_{SL}$, i.e.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma-equilibrium}
\sigma_A^* = \frac{1}{\gamma_\sigma}\left(1 - R_0^{-1}\right),
\end{equation}
the same expression as $\sigma_{\text{critical}}$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma-critical}): the coherent equilibrium sits exactly
on the threshold value of the emerging branch. Since $\sigma_A^*$ can
exceed $1$ when $R_0 > 1/(1-\gamma_\sigma)$, the attractor of the
clamped dynamics is $\min(\sigma_A^*, 1)$. Local stability follows from
the linearisation of the two-dimensional Jacobian at each equilibrium.
The trivial equilibrium $E_0$ exists iff the effective training input
vanishes ($f_{\text{learn}} \cdot T_A = 0$): under the Gompertz form
$\eta(0) = \eta_{\max} e^{-a} > 0$, so $\dot{\delta}_A > 0$ at
$\delta_A = 0$ whenever training input is nonzero. $\square$

\paragraph*{Proof of Proposition 3 (low-$\sigma$ trap: global
stability).}
The bracket $B(\sigma_A) = \rho P_A \alpha_A (1 - \gamma_\sigma
\sigma_A) - \epsilon_\sigma \Omega_{SL}$ is maximised at
$\sigma_A = 0$, where $B(0) = \rho P_A \alpha_A - \epsilon_\sigma
\Omega_{SL} < 0$ iff $R_0 < 1$. Since $\dot{\sigma}_A = \sigma_A
B(\sigma_A)$ with $\sigma_A > 0$ on $(0,1]$, we have
$\dot{\sigma}_A < 0$ on the whole interval, so $\sigma_A$ decreases
monotonically to $0$ from any initial state in $(0,1]$. $\square$

\paragraph*{Proof of Proposition 4 ($\sigma_{\text{critical}}$ and the
transcritical bifurcation).}
Linearising the $\sigma_A$ ODE around $\sigma_A = 0$:
\begin{align}
\dot{\sigma}_A &= \sigma_A\bigl[\rho P_A\alpha_A(1-\gamma_\sigma\sigma_A) - \epsilon_\sigma\Omega_{SL}\bigr] \notag\\
&= r_{\text{net}}\,\sigma_A - k\,\sigma_A^2,
\label{eq:bif-normal-form}
\end{align}
with $r_{\text{net}} = \rho P_A \alpha_A - \epsilon_\sigma \Omega_{SL}$
(which changes sign at $R_0 = 1$) and $k = \gamma_\sigma \rho P_A
\alpha_A > 0$ --- the normal form of a transcritical bifurcation.
Writing $F(\sigma_A; R_0) = \dot{\sigma}_A$, the conditions for a
transcritical bifurcation at $R_0 = 1$ are: the trivial branch persists
for all parameters, $F(0; R_0) \equiv 0$; $\partial_\sigma F(0; 1) = 0$;
the mixed partial $\partial_\sigma \partial_{R_0} F(0; 1) =
\epsilon_\sigma \Omega_{SL} \neq 0$; and
$\partial^2_{\sigma\sigma} F(0; 1) = -2\gamma_\sigma \rho P_A \alpha_A
\neq 0$. The parameter derivative $\partial_{R_0} F$ vanishes at the
bifurcation point $\sigma_A = 0$ --- precisely the signature of a
transcritical (exchange-of-stability) bifurcation, in which the trivial
branch persists, rather than a saddle-node bifurcation. The trivial
branch $\sigma_A = 0$ is stable for $r_{\text{net}} < 0$ ($R_0 < 1$) and
unstable for $r_{\text{net}} > 0$ ($R_0 > 1$); the non-trivial branch
$\sigma_A^* = r_{\text{net}}/k$ exists and is stable for $R_0 > 1$. The
threshold follows from $\sigma_A^* > 0 \iff 1 - R_0^{-1} > 0$. $\square$
