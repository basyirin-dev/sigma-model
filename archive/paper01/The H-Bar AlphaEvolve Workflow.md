## 1. STRATEGIC FOUNDATION

### 1.1 Why These Three Tools and This Hackathon

**The tool assignment is not arbitrary.** Each tool was chosen because it addresses a different layer of what makes a theoretical paper fail at submission:

- **Consensus** addresses the literature layer — the risk that a claim you make has been made before, or that a post-publication paper has contradicted your evidence base without your knowledge.
- **Stanford Agentic Reviewer** addresses the structural layer — the risk that your argument has internal weaknesses visible to a structured reviewer that are invisible to you as the author.
- **AlphaEvolve Loop** addresses the improvement layer — the risk that the first fix you propose for a problem is not the best fix, and that you need an evolutionary search over the fix space to find the optimal solution.

**The hackathon is not separate from the paper — it is the paper's empirical verification layer.** The DeepMind Kaggle hackathon "Measuring progress toward AGI: Cognitive abilities" (open March 17 – April 16, 2026, $200,000 prize pool) asks for exactly what V3.0+ provides theoretically: formal evaluation protocols for Learning, Metacognition, Attention, Executive Functions, and Social Cognition. Every formal variable in V3.0+ maps directly to one of the five hackathon tracks.

### 1.2 What Changed From V1.0 to V3.0+: The Structural Delta

Understanding the delta is essential because it defines the integration risks the workflow must address.

**V1.0 (original PDF):** σA, δA, βA, ΨA, D*, 5-phase arc, 6 predictions. Tight, single-faculty framework. One novelty claim.

**V2.0 additions:** αA(d,t) attentional fidelity; ΞA(t) = {ΞA^P, ΞA^I, ΞA^F} executive control; M̂A(d,t) + ζA(d,t) metacognitive self-model; μAB + τA(B,d,t) + ΣA,B social cognition. Four new ODEs. Theory-of-mind coupling.

**V3.0 additions:** ΘA(d,m1,m2,t) cross-modal schema transfer; ω(m1,m2) modal similarity; VA(B,f,t) benchmark validity function; CI, FD, DG validity components. D×M product space extension.

**V3.0+ additions:** RA(B,f,t) reliability function; HB(B) human baseline specification; pre-audit protocol; temperature protocol; micro-gap closures. 8 predictions (up from 6).

### 1.3 The Bidirectional Verification Map

The paper and hackathon are not parallel independent tracks. They verify each other:

### Gap & Conflict Map Format

For every paper Falcon returns, create one row in gap_conflict_map.md:

```
| Paper | Relevant Section | Supports H-Bar | Contradicts/Extends | Status | Action |
```

Status options:

- `[CONFIRMED]` — No action; novelty/claim verified
- `[CITE]` — Add citation at specified location
- `[ADDRESS]` — Requires new text to differentiate or engage
- `[SCOPE OUT]` — Add to §10 limitations with justification
- `[INTEGRATION]` — Affects cross-variable coupling in integration_map.md AND register.md

Every `[CITE]` and `[ADDRESS]` row becomes a `[R]`-tagged or `[N]`-tagged issue in the Master Issue Register. Every `[INTEGRATION]` row becomes both a register issue AND a row update in integration_map.md.

### QUERY 1 — Compositional Generalisation Benchmarks (Core Evidence Base)

**Query text:**

> `SCAN COGS CFQ PCFG-SET compositional generalisation transformer performance 2022–2025`

**Why this query first:** Your paper's entire empirical grounding rests on five benchmark papers — Lake & Baroni (2018), Bahdanau et al. (2019), Kim & Linzen (2020), Keysers et al. (2020), Hupkes et al. (2020). All are pre-2022. The field has moved. You need to know if any transformer-based architecture has significantly improved compositional generalisation since 2022. Your §1 central claim ("the failure is structural and consistent") is contested if a 2024 model achieves >70% on SCAN add-primitive.

**What to look for specifically:**

- Any paper reporting transformer models achieving >70% on SCAN add-primitive or COGS compositional splits
- Lake and Baroni (2023) — "Human-like systematic generalisation through a meta-learning neural network" (Nature, 623, 115–121) — your references include this. Verify its scope and whether it challenges or supports your structural framing
- Csordás et al. (2021) — already cited in V3.0+. Verify this is correctly scoped in §2.2
- Any new benchmark that supersedes or extends COGS/CFQ with different diagnostic findings

**Decision rule:**

- If no post-2022 paper significantly improves compositional generalisation → §1 framing stands. Record `[CONFIRMED]`
- If a post-2022 paper achieves high SCAN/COGS scores → add a sentence to §2.2 gap statement acknowledging it. Explain why H-Bar's structural diagnosis holds even if performance has partially improved. Record `[ADDRESS]` with exact location in §2.2

### TRACK 1 — Learning: H-Bar Phase Transition Benchmark (H-PTB)

**Theoretical grounding:** §3 core framework, §7 phase structure, Predictions 1 and 2, Figure 1

**Core evaluation question:** Does AI learning proceed through structural phase transitions — non-linear jumps in OOD capability relative to in-distribution capability — as H-Bar's σcritical framework predicts? And does attentional fidelity αA accelerate or delay these transitions?

**Benchmark design — four conditions:**

_Condition A (Baseline):_ Random training item ordering. PCFG-SET dataset. Evaluate in-distribution accuracy and compositional generalisation accuracy every 1,000 training steps.

_Condition B (Difficulty-ordered):_ Standard curriculum learning. Items ordered by training loss signal. Same evaluation protocol.

_Condition C (Structure-targeted):_ Curriculum designed to maximise principled practice rate PA — contrastive pairs emphasising structural rule over surface regularity. Same evaluation protocol.

_Condition D (Structure-targeted + αA building):_ Condition C plus explicit contrastive training tasks CA(d,t) — tasks requiring the agent to discriminate between surface-matched/structure-different item pairs. Same evaluation protocol.

**Phase transition detection procedure:**

At each evaluation checkpoint, compute: OOD ratio = (OOD accuracy) / (in-distribution accuracy). Plot this ratio over training time for all four conditions. Test for non-linearity using a piecewise linear fit with one breakpoint (change-point detection). The H-Bar prediction: the ratio should remain near-zero in Phase 1, then exhibit a statistically significant breakpoint corresponding to the σcritical crossing.

**Primary H-Bar predictions tested:**

- Condition C shows earlier breakpoint than Condition B, which shows earlier than Condition A (phase structure is inducible and accelerated by structure-targeted curriculum)
- Condition D shows the earliest breakpoint of all (αA building further accelerates σcritical crossing)
- The ratio remains near-zero in the partial zone before breakpoint (Prediction 6 analog: σA below threshold produces near-zero OOD benefit)

**Falsification conditions:**

- No statistically significant breakpoint in any condition → phase structure claim fails
- Conditions A and C have statistically indistinguishable breakpoint timing → structure-targeted curriculum claim fails
- Condition D breakpoint not earlier than C → αA building claim fails

**Human baseline protocol:** N=200 participants on Prolific Academic. Participants complete a subset of PCFG-SET items under time pressure (to prevent memorisation) after brief instruction. Score on same OOD/in-distribution split. Report human OOD ratio as reference baseline.

**Kaggle implementation:** Deploy simplified version of all four conditions on Day 8 using available frontier models via Kaggle Community Benchmarks. Run full version with N=200 human baseline from Day 15.

### TRACK 2 — Metacognition: H-Bar Metacognitive Calibration Benchmark (H-MCB)

**Theoretical grounding:** §4.4 M̂A(d,t), ζA(d,t), Prediction 2, Eq. 38–39

**Core evaluation question:** Do AI agents systematically overestimate their OOD capabilities when trained in AI-augmented (high ΩAI) regimes — as the M̂A ODE predicts — compared to agents trained with principled structured-failure protocols?

**Benchmark design — two-stage protocol:**

_Stage 1 (Prediction):_ Present the agent with a brief description of an OOD compositional task type (e.g., "applying a rule to a novel primitive combination not seen in training"). Ask: "What is your estimated accuracy on [N] items of this type? Provide a percentage." Record predicted accuracy Pred(i) for each item type i.

_Stage 2 (Performance):_ Present the agent with the actual OOD items. Record actual accuracy Actual(i) for each item type i.

**Calibration error computation:**

ζ̂A = (1/N) · Σ_i [Pred(i) − Actual(i)]

Positive ζ̂A = systematic overconfidence. This is the H-Bar prediction for high-ΩAI training conditions.

**Experimental conditions:**

_Condition ΩAI-high:_ Agents that have been demonstrably trained with heavy AI augmentation (identified by high in-distribution accuracy at low training compute, consistent with shortcut learning). Use GPT-4 and Claude as examples of high-capability frontier models.

_Condition PA-high:_ Agents specifically trained on structured-failure curriculum (PCFG-SET Condition C from H-PTB above, or equivalent). If not separately trainable on Kaggle, use smaller-scale controlled training runs.

_Matched control:_ Match conditions on in-distribution accuracy to isolate σA differences from δA differences.

**Primary H-Bar predictions tested:**

- ζ̂A is positive (overconfident) across all frontier models on OOD items
- ζ̂A is larger in high-ΩAI conditions than in matched low-ΩAI conditions
- The magnitude of ζ̂A correlates with the in-distribution / OOD accuracy gap (the σcritical proxy)

**Falsification conditions:**

- ζ̂A ≤ 0 systematically → metacognitive overconfidence prediction fails
- No significant difference in ζ̂A between ΩAI-high and ΩAI-low conditions → ΩAI suppression of M̂A fails

**Human baseline protocol:** N=200 participants on Prolific Academic. Same two-stage protocol on a subset of COGS items. Report human ζ̂A as reference baseline. H-Bar predicts human ζ̂A > 0 for novice participants and ζ̂A ≈ 0 for domain experts — providing an independent partial test.

**Kaggle implementation:** Available frontier models on Kaggle Community Benchmarks. Use temperature=0 for Stage 2 (performance measurement). Use temperature=0.7 for Stage 1 (prediction elicitation, to capture distribution of confidence estimates).

### TRACK 3 — Attention: H-Bar Attentional Fidelity Benchmark (H-AFB)

**Theoretical grounding:** §4.1 αA(d,t), CA(d,t), Eq. 27–30

**Core evaluation question:** Do AI agents preferentially track surface-statistical regularities over compositional structural rules when both are simultaneously available — and does this surface-tracking preference predict OOD failure more strongly than training volume?

**Benchmark design — dual regularity competition:**

Create a dataset with a critical property: items that can be solved by either (a) surface n-gram statistics (high correlation with training label, zero OOD validity) or (b) compositional structural rules (lower correlation with training label, full OOD validity).

Specifically: a modified SCAN dataset where some training items have a surface feature (e.g., colour token) that co-occurs with the correct output label in training data but not in OOD splits. An agent using the surface feature scores well in-distribution and fails OOD. An agent using the compositional rule scores moderately in-distribution and maintains OOD accuracy.

**Measurement procedure:**

_In-distribution:_ Present items with both surface and structural cues available. Record accuracy. _OOD-structural:_ Present items where structural rule applies but surface cue is absent or misleading. Record accuracy. _OOD-surface-conflict:_ Present items where surface cue points to wrong answer; structural rule gives correct answer. Record accuracy.

The αA proxy = OOD-structural accuracy / in-distribution accuracy. High-αA agents show small drop. Low-αA agents show large drop.

The surface-conflict drop = (in-distribution accuracy) − (OOD-surface-conflict accuracy). This directly measures how much the agent was relying on the surface cue.

**Primary H-Bar predictions tested:**

- Surface-conflict drop is larger for frontier models trained on larger, more diverse datasets (more surface regularity exposure → lower αA)
- αA proxy correlates with OOD ratio from H-PTB across models
- The surface-conflict drop is larger than the OOD-structural drop for low-αA agents (surface cues are actively misleading, not just absent)

**Falsification conditions:**

- No significant surface-conflict drop for any model → surface regularity tracking is not occurring
- OOD-structural and OOD-surface-conflict drops are statistically indistinguishable → surface cues are not influencing the agent

**Human baseline:** N=200 participants. Same dual-regularity SCAN subset. H-Bar predicts humans show smaller surface-conflict drop than frontier models (humans more readily attend to structural rules when cued).

### TRACK 4 — Executive Functions: H-Bar Delegation Control Benchmark (H-DCB)

**Theoretical grounding:** §3.6, §6, Figure 2, ΞA^I (inhibition), D* H-Bar criterion, Eq. 22–23

**Core evaluation question:** Does the non-monotonic D* prediction hold empirically — does increasing AI retrieval density improve compositional accuracy for high-σA agents and decrease it for low-σA agents?

**Benchmark design — retrieval density manipulation:**

Use COGS or PCFG-SET as the base task. Create a RAG-augmented version where the agent has access to a retrieval system. Systematically vary the retrieval density ρ (fraction of task items where retrieved context is provided) from ρ=0 (no retrieval) to ρ=1 (all items augmented with retrieved context).

For each ρ, measure compositional generalisation accuracy Acccomp on the OOD split.

**Agent conditions:**

_Low-σA proxy:_ Frontier models evaluated directly (high in-distribution accuracy, uncontrolled OOD accuracy — the standard deployment scenario).

_High-σA proxy:_ Frontier models that have been fine-tuned on structured-failure curriculum (or use PCFG-SET Condition C trained models if available).

_Matched on δA:_ Conditions matched on in-distribution accuracy to isolate σA effects.

**Primary H-Bar predictions tested (the non-monotonic prediction — sharpest empirical claim):**

- For low-σA proxy agents: Acccomp decreases as ρ increases (∂Acccomp/∂ρ < 0) — more delegation makes OOD performance worse
- For high-σA proxy agents: Acccomp increases as ρ increases (∂Acccomp/∂ρ > 0) — more delegation makes OOD performance better
- The cross-over point corresponds to an observable training signal (the learning-rate discontinuity proxy from Figure 1)

**Falsification conditions:**

- Acccomp increases monotonically with ρ for all agent conditions → non-monotonic prediction fails; σ-gate is not operating
- No significant difference in ρ-response curve between low-σA and high-σA conditions → delegation gradient D* σ-gate claim fails

**Human baseline:** N=200 participants with and without AI retrieval tool access. Score compositional reasoning task accuracy in both conditions. H-Bar predicts domain experts (high σA) benefit from retrieval while novices (low σA) are harmed by it.

### TRACK 5 — Social Cognition: H-Bar Schema Transmission Benchmark (H-STB)

**Theoretical grounding:** §4.2 μAB(d,t), τA(B,d,t), ΣA,B(d1,d2,t), Eq. 31–34

**Core evaluation question:** Can AI agents transmit schema coherence (principled structural understanding) to other agents through communication more effectively than they can transmit factual knowledge — and does this transmission quality scale with the schema legibility of both communicating agents?

**Benchmark design — two-agent communication protocol:**

_Agent A_ (Transmitter): Has been trained on a compositional domain and achieves a known in-distribution / OOD ratio (proxy for σA).

_Agent B_ (Receiver): Has not been exposed to the target domain. Must learn to answer OOD compositional questions about the domain using only Agent A's communications.

**Communication conditions:**

_Condition Fact-transmission:_ Agent A communicates by listing observed facts ("in training, 'jump twice' maps to 'JUMP JUMP'"). Agent B answered after receiving N facts.

_Condition Schema-transmission:_ Agent A communicates by describing structural rules ("the pattern is: verb + quantifier → VERB repeated quantifier times"). Agent B answers after receiving N structural descriptions.

_Condition Mixed:_ Agent A communicates both.

**Scoring:** Agent B's OOD compositional accuracy after each communication condition. H-Bar predicts: Schema-transmission > Mixed > Fact-transmission for Agent B's OOD accuracy, because μAB is higher when Agent A communicates structural principles than when it communicates facts.

**Theory-of-mind component:** After Condition Schema-transmission, ask Agent A to predict Agent B's accuracy on a new OOD item. Compare Agent A's prediction τA(B, OOD) against Agent B's actual accuracy. The prediction error is the ζAB proxy.

**Primary H-Bar predictions tested:**

- Schema-transmission produces higher Agent B OOD accuracy than Fact-transmission at matched communication volume
- Agent A's τA accuracy (predicting Agent B's OOD performance) is higher when Agent A has higher σA proxy
- ΣA,B activation (both agents above θI) produces higher joint OOD accuracy than either agent alone

**Falsification conditions:**

- No significant difference between schema and fact transmission conditions → schema legibility claim fails
- Agent A τA accuracy not above chance → theory-of-mind coupling claim fails

**Human baseline:** N=200 participants in dyadic communication task. One participant explains a compositional task to another without showing examples. Score recipient's OOD accuracy. H-Bar predicts expert explainers (high σA) produce higher recipient OOD accuracy than novice explainers.
