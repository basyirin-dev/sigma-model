# Comprehensive Research Methodology Checklist

**Purpose:** A pre-submission quality assurance instrument covering topic selection through final writing.  
**Structure:** 5 Parts, 27 sections, ~400+ items.  
**Usage:** Work through each section before submitting your proposal, thesis, or manuscript.

---

## Part I: Research Foundation

---

### I-A: Topic Selection

Based on: FINER criteria, viability & significance assessment, topic approval strategies.

```
[ ] I-A-01: Is my topic specific and contextual, not vague or broad?
    (Not "social media" but "the impact of TikTok on body image among Gen Z women in the UK")
[ ] I-A-02: Have I tested my topic against the FINER criteria?
    (Feasible, Interesting, Novel, Ethical, Relevant)
[ ] I-A-03: Is it feasible? Do I have the time, funding, expertise, and technology?
    (Be brutally honest — if it fails here, it fails later and expensively)
[ ] I-A-04: Do I have access to the data or population I need?
    (Can I reach the institutions, respondents, or datasets?)
[ ] I-A-05: Can I complete this without relying on resources outside my control?
    (Independence of execution)
[ ] I-A-06: Is the scope manageable within my timeframe and word count?
    (Too broad → unfocused searches and messy structure)
[ ] I-A-07: Is it novel? Does it offer a new angle, method, context, or population?
    (Novelty can be: new question, new method, new context, new synthesis)
[ ] I-A-08: Have I distinguished "no literature" from a "research gap"?
    (No literature usually means not viable — reframe to connect to existing work)
[ ] I-A-09: Is it relevant to my field, practitioners, or society?
    (Does it matter beyond academic curiosity?)
[ ] I-A-10: Am I genuinely interested and excited to work on this for months/years?
    (Sustained momentum requires genuine curiosity)
[ ] I-A-11: Have I considered my long-term career goals in this choice?
    (Does this topic open doors you want to walk through?)
[ ] I-A-12: Have I aligned my topic with my discipline's tools, methods, and expectations?
    (Does it reflect the language and conventions of your field?)
[ ] I-A-13: Have I discussed this topic with my supervisor or potential supervisor?
    (Alignment with their expertise ensures good guidance)
[ ] I-A-14: Have I checked course objectives to ensure the topic fits?
    (Does your professor/department approve this area?)
[ ] I-A-15: Did I start with a real-world problem, not just a lack of research?
    ("We don't know enough about X" is not a valid problem statement)
[ ] I-A-16: Have I asked "the right questions" before committing?
    (Is it specific? Is there a clear gap? Does it fit my course?)
[ ] I-A-17: Have I done a quick database search to check for academic coverage?
    (Look for depth, not just a handful of sources)
[ ] I-A-18: Have I applied the "So What?" test?
    (If I answer this question, who cares and why?)
[ ] I-A-19: Is my topic intellectually challenging but potentially achievable?
    (Not incremental/minor, but not impossible)
[ ] I-A-20: Have I considered whether this topic aligns with my supervisor's research?
    (If no alignment → slow progress, weak guidance)
[ ] I-A-21: Have I identified the specific gap this topic fills?
    (Evidence gap, knowledge gap, methodological gap, etc.)
[ ] I-A-22: Will this topic contribute to existing knowledge, not just repeat it?
    (Extend, refine, test, or challenge existing work)
[ ] I-A-23: Have I considered the "grand challenge" dimension?
    (Does it address a significant real-world or theoretical problem?)
[ ] I-A-24: Is my topic ethical? Does it protect participants and adhere to guidelines?
[ ] I-A-25: Have I considered writing a concept note first to get early feedback?
```

### I-B: Research Philosophy & Paradigms

Based on: Positivism → Post-positivism → Interpretivism → Critical Theory → Pragmatism → Critical Realism.

```
[ ] I-B-01: Have I identified my research paradigm explicitly?
    (Positivism, Post-positivism, Interpretivism, Critical Theory, Pragmatism, etc.)
[ ] I-B-02: Have I considered my ontological position?
    (Realism — objective reality exists; Relativism — reality is socially constructed)
[ ] I-B-03: Have I considered my epistemological position?
    (Objectivism — knowledge discovered; Subjectivism — knowledge interpreted)
[ ] I-B-04: Have I considered my axiological position?
    (Value-free, value-aware, or value-laden?)
[ ] I-B-05: Have I ensured consistency across ontology, epistemology, axiology, and methodology?
    (They must logically align — e.g., positivism + qualitative methods is inconsistent)
[ ] I-B-06: Does my paradigm match my research question?
    (Testing hypotheses → positivism; understanding meanings → interpretivism)
[ ] I-B-07: Have I considered whether my paradigm aligns with my worldview?
    (Congruency with your philosophical view of life)
[ ] I-B-08: Have I justified my paradigm choice in my proposal?
    (Explain why this paradigm is appropriate for your study)
[ ] I-B-09: Have I considered alternativparadigms and stated why they are less suitable?
[ ] I-B-10: If using pragmatism, have I articulated the practical rationale?
    (Focus on "what works" to answer the question)
[ ] I-B-11: Have I considered the implications of my paradigm for my methods?
    (Paradigm → methodology → methods — all must align)
[ ] I-B-12: Have I been reflexive about how my values influence my research?
    (Especially important for interpretivist and critical paradigms)
[ ] I-B-13: Have I explicitly stated my paradigm in my methodology chapter?
[ ] I-B-14: Have I discussed my philosophical assumptions with my supervisor?
[ ] I-B-15: Have I read key methodological texts on my chosen paradigm?
    (e.g., Creswell for interpretivism, Comte for positivism, Bhaskar for critical realism)
[ ] I-B-16: If using critical theory, have I articulated the emancipatory goal?
    (How does this research challenge power and change society?)
[ ] I-B-17: Have I avoided using paradigms as rigid templates?
    (Encourage ontological/epistemological discussion, not formulaic labelling)
[ ] I-B-18: Does my methodology chapter demonstrate awareness of the paradigm debate?
[ ] I-B-19: Have I considered the axiological dimension of my data interpretation?
    (How do my values shape what I see in the data?)
[ ] I-B-20: If doing mixed methods, have I considered pragmatism as a philosophical foundation?
```

### I-C: Theoretical Framework

Based on: IMPACT framework, when to choose, guiding questions.

```
[ ] I-C-01: Have I started thinking about my theoretical framework early?
    (After preliminary research questions, before full data collection)
[ ] I-C-02: Did my theoretical framework emerge from my literature review?
    (It is a compilation of the thoughts of "giants in your field")
[ ] I-C-03: Have I mapped the theoretical landscape?
    (Become familiar with multiple perspectives that could inform your work)
[ ] I-C-04: Have I applied the IMPACT framework to evaluate my choice?
    (Interestingness, Matching, Parsimony, Applicability, Conceptual rigour, Testability)
[ ] I-C-05: Interestingness: Does the theory challenge prior findings or offer novelty?
[ ] I-C-06: Matching: Does it align with the specific concepts/entities I am studying?
[ ] I-C-07: Parsimony: Is it as simple as possible while still explaining the phenomenon?
[ ] I-C-08: Applicability: Can it be practically applied to my research context?
[ ] I-C-09: Conceptual Rigour: Is it well-developed, clearly defined, and logically sound?
[ ] I-C-10: Testability: Can it be tested or supported through my research design?
[ ] I-C-11: Have I considered subjective criteria?
    (Personal comfort, congruency with my worldview, sociopolitical context)
[ ] I-C-12: Have I considered objective criteria?
    (Theorist's standing, availability of writings, utility and ease of use)
[ ] I-C-13: Have I distinguished theoretical from conceptual framework?
    (Theoretical = pre-existing theory; Conceptual = built from multiple concepts)
[ ] I-C-14: Have I clarified whether my research is deductive or inductive?
    (Deductive → theoretical framework; Inductive → conceptual framework)
[ ] I-C-15: Have I asked: "Why will my research benefit from this framework?"
[ ] I-C-16: Have I asked: "How does this framework align with my worldview?"
[ ] I-C-17: Can I clearly articulate why this framework is the best one for my study?
[ ] I-C-18: Is my framework grounded in credible, cited literature?
[ ] I-C-19: Have I considered the framework as a "lens" that shapes my entire project?
    (Different theories bring different aspects into focus)
[ ] I-C-20: Have I discussed my framework choice with my supervisor?
```

### I-D: Problem Statement

Based on: Vision-Reality-Gap-Consequence model, 4 components, when to write.

```
[ ] I-D-01: Did I draft my problem statement early and finalize it last?
    (Start early to guide your work; revise at the end for accuracy)
[ ] I-D-02: Did I conduct a thorough literature review before finalizing my problem?
    (A gap is identified through literature, not intuition)
[ ] I-D-03: Have I specified and described the problem clearly?
    (State the problem with appropriate citations)
[ ] I-D-04: Have I provided evidence of the problem's existence?
    (Support claims with current research findings and statistics)
[ ] I-D-05: Have I explained the consequences of NOT solving the problem?
    (The "So What?" — why does this matter urgently?)
[ ] I-D-06: Have I explained the knowledge needed to solve the problem (the gap)?
    (What is currently unknown that needs to be discovered?)
[ ] I-D-07: Is my problem specific, not just a topic?
    (Topic: "Self-compassion." Problem: "Trauma survivors cannot practice self-compassion effectively")
[ ] I-D-08: Is my problem evidence-based, not anecdotal?
    (Quantitative evidence from credible sources is preferred)
[ ] I-D-09: Is my problem a real-life issue, not just a lack of research?
    ("Lack of research" alone is not a valid problem statement)
[ ] I-D-10: Have I used the Vision-Reality-Gap-Consequence structure?
    (Should be → But → Missing Link → So What)
[ ] I-D-11: Is my problem focused on one specific issue?
    (Or a very small number of closely related ones)
[ ] I-D-12: Is the language direct, factual, and academic?
    (Avoid jargon, subjective language, "may," "might," "perhaps")
[ ] I-D-13: Have I avoided broad generalities like "never" and "always"?
[ ] I-D-14: Is the problem challenging and original but potentially achievable?
[ ] I-D-15: Is my problem statement concise (often one page or less)?
[ ] I-D-16: Have I used the 5 Ws (What, Where, Why, Who, When) to cover context?
[ ] I-D-17: Can I start with the sentence: "The problem to be addressed in this study is..."?
```

### I-E: Research Questions, Aims & Objectives

Based on: SMART framework, hierarchy (aim → questions → objectives), FINER.

```
[ ] I-E-01: Do I understand the hierarchy? (Aim → Research Questions → Objectives)
[ ] I-E-02: Is my aim a single, overarching mission statement?
    (One aim, maybe two — not more)
[ ] I-E-03: Does my aim use an infinitive verb?
    (To examine, to investigate, to determine, to explore, to evaluate...)
[ ] I-E-04: Does my aim answer Why? What? and How?
[ ] I-E-05: Is my aim written in the present tense?
[ ] I-E-06: Are my research questions focused on a single problem each?
[ ] I-E-07: Are my questions researchable using primary or secondary sources?
[ ] I-E-08: Are my questions feasible within my timeframe and constraints?
[ ] I-E-09: Are my questions specific enough to be answered thoroughly?
[ ] I-E-10: Are my questions complex enough to develop over a full thesis?
[ ] I-E-11: Are my questions relevant to my field and/or society?
[ ] I-E-12: Are my questions ethical?
[ ] I-E-13: Have I tested my questions against the FINER criteria?
    (Feasible, Interesting, Novel, Ethical, Relevant)
[ ] I-E-14: Are my objectives specific — clearly stating what I will do?
[ ] I-E-15: Are my objectives measurable — can I track progress?
[ ] I-E-16: Are my objectives achievable within my capabilities?
[ ] I-E-17: Are my objectives relevant — directly related to my aim?
[ ] I-E-18: Are my objectives time-bound — completable within my timeline?
[ ] I-E-19: Do I have 3-5 objectives? (Not more, not fewer)
[ ] I-E-20: Do my objectives start with strong action verbs?
    (Determine, assess, compare, evaluate, analyse — NOT "understand" or "study")
[ ] I-E-21: Do my objectives build on each other logically?
    (Each one should flow from the previous)
[ ] I-E-22: Have I aligned my objectives with my research questions?
    (They are the same content — objectives as statements, questions as questions)
[ ] I-E-23: Have I avoided having too many aims or objectives?
    (Project becomes unwieldy and unfocused)
[ ] I-E-24: Have I avoided vague verbs like "understand" or "explore"?
    (Use "determine," "measure," "compare," "evaluate")
[ ] I-E-25: Will my aims, questions, and objectives appear near the end of my introduction?
```

## Part II: Literature Review

---

### II-A: Review Type Selection

Based on: 12 types of research reviews and their appropriate uses.

```
[ ] II-A-01: Have I asked "What is my goal?" — this determines the review type
[ ] II-A-02: Systematic review — am I answering a clear, focused question with strongest evidence?
    (Use when: you need rigorous, reproducible evidence synthesis)
[ ] II-A-03: Scoping review — am I exploring a broad or emerging topic?
    (Use when: mapping gaps and trends in under-explored areas)
[ ] II-A-04: Narrative review — am I summarizing and interpreting to tell the "story" of a topic?
    (Use when: theory building, essays, broad overviews)
[ ] II-A-05: Rapid review — is time tight but I still need reliable evidence?
    (Use when: policy timelines demand quick synthesis)
[ ] II-A-06: Umbrella review — am I combining multiple systematic reviews?
    (Use when: seeing the big picture across topics)
[ ] II-A-07: Integrative review — does my topic include quantitative + qualitative studies?
    (Use when: needing a full perspective from mixed evidence types)
[ ] II-A-08: Realist review — do I want to know how or why something works in different contexts?
    (Use when: understanding mechanisms in real-world settings)
[ ] II-A-09: Qualitative review — am I exploring experiences, beliefs, or meanings?
    (Use when: synthesizing multiple qualitative studies)
[ ] II-A-10: Critical review — am I challenging existing ideas and offering new conceptual takes?
    (Use when: the goal is to provoke new thinking)
[ ] II-A-11: State-of-the-art review — am I highlighting cutting-edge developments?
    (Use when: the field is moving fast and needs an update)
[ ] II-A-12: Bibliometric review — am I analyzing publication/citation patterns?
    (Use when: studying how the field itself is evolving)
[ ] II-A-13: Mapping review — am I visually organizing what's been studied?
    (Use when: spotting where research is missing)
[ ] II-A-14: Have I clearly stated which review type I am conducting in my methodology?
[ ] II-A-15: Have I justified why this review type is appropriate for my research question?
```

---

### II-B: Literature Search

Based on: Boolean operators, citation chaining, databases, keyword strategy.

```
[ ] II-B-01: Have I defined a clear, focused research question before searching?
[ ] II-B-02: Have I broken my topic into main concepts and brainstormed synonyms?
[ ] II-B-03: Have I used Boolean operators correctly?
    (AND to narrow, OR to broaden, NOT to exclude)
[ ] II-B-04: Have I searched multiple academic databases relevant to my discipline?
[ ] II-B-05: Have I checked course reading lists for key authors and theories?
[ ] II-B-06: Have I used Google Scholar or Scopus for broader searching?
[ ] II-B-07: Have I used citation chaining — forward and backward?
    (Examine references of on-point articles; check who has cited them)
[ ] II-B-08: Have I looked for seminal works — highly cited, foundational to the field?
[ ] II-B-09: Have I considered multiple source types?
    (Journal articles, books, dissertations, reports — depends on discipline)
[ ] II-B-10: Is my search systematic and documented?
    (Treat the literature search like an experiment)
[ ] II-B-11: Have I kept a search log with databases, keywords, and date ranges?
[ ] II-B-12: Have I considered using citation management software from the start?
    (Zotero, Mendeley — organize as you go)
[ ] II-B-13: Have I checked whether my search is too broad or too narrow?
    (Too much literature → narrow scope; too little → broaden)
[ ] II-B-14: Have I set inclusion and exclusion criteria for my search results?
[ ] II-B-15: Have I used the abstract to quickly determine relevance?
    (Read abstract first; the paper doesn't have to align perfectly)
```

### II-C: Critical Evaluation & Synthesis

Based on: PEER system, synthesizing vs. summarizing, literature matrix, critical analysis.

```
[ ] II-C-01: Have I moved beyond description to critical analysis?
    (Not just reporting, but evaluating and interpreting)
[ ] II-C-02: Do I balance critique — acknowledging strengths AND weaknesses?
    (Many focus only on flaws — show fairness and depth)
[ ] II-C-03: Have I synthesized findings rather than summarizing them one by one?
    (Integration ≠ list of article summaries)
[ ] II-C-04: Have I avoided the "summary trap"?
    (Quote dumping, citation stacking, one-source paragraphs)
[ ] II-C-05: Have I identified patterns, contradictions, and gaps across sources?
[ ] II-C-06: Have I structured my review around a clear argument, not a topic list?
    (Not "Definitions → Causes → Effects" but a driving purpose)
[ ] II-C-07: Have I chosen an organizing strategy?
    (Thematic, methodological, or chronological — not source-by-source)
[ ] II-C-08: Does every section of my review have a one-sentence purpose?
    (What will this section prove or clarify?)
[ ] II-C-09: Have I used the PEER system for each paragraph?
    (Point → Explanation → Example/Evidence → Relate/Reiterate)
[ ] II-C-10: P — Does each paragraph start with my own claim?
    (Not "Smith (2023) says..." but "Digital transformation is driven by leadership vision...")
[ ] II-C-11: E — Have I explained the logic or context behind my point?
[ ] II-C-12: E — Have I used evidence from multiple sources, not just one?
[ ] II-C-13: E — Have I synthesized the evidence, showing agreement or disagreement?
    ("While Johnson emphasizes X, Lee and Patel argue Y...")
[ ] II-C-14: R — Does each paragraph link back to my research question?
    (The "So What?" — what does this mean for my study?)
[ ] II-C-15: Have I used a "Literature Matrix" to track sources?
    (Spreadsheet with methodology, findings, strengths, weaknesses)
[ ] II-C-16: Have I critically evaluated each source for credibility and relevance?
[ ] II-C-17: Have I read the abstract first to determine relevance?
[ ] II-C-18: Have I recorded key points, similarities, differences, and my reactions?
    (My reactions in a separate column — basis for critical evaluation)
[ ] II-C-19: Have I avoided treating all sources as equally strong?
    (Note limitations, biases, and methodological weaknesses)
[ ] II-C-20: Does my review tell a coherent story?
    (Take the reader from basics to sophisticated advances)
[ ] II-C-21: Have I maintained my own voice throughout?
    (You are the ambassador for the subject, not just a summarizer)
[ ] II-C-22: Have I kept language clear for non-expert readers?
[ ] II-C-23: Have I used citation management software throughout?
```

### II-D: Gap Identification

Based on: 7 types of research gaps.

```
[ ] II-D-01: Have I clearly identified the type of gap my research fills?
[ ] II-D-02: Evidence Gap — is there insufficient or outdated evidence?
    (Opportunity: conduct new experiments, collect fresh data, replicate)
[ ] II-D-03: Knowledge Gap — are certain topics or aspects unexplored?
    (Opportunity: explore new areas, apply theories to novel contexts)
[ ] II-D-04: Practical-Knowledge Conflict Gap — does theory contradict practice?
    (Opportunity: bridge research and practice, validate/update frameworks)
[ ] II-D-05: Methodological Gap — did prior studies use limited or outdated methods?
    (Opportunity: apply new tools, technologies, or approaches)
[ ] II-D-06: Empirical Gap — are there inconsistencies in existing findings?
    (Opportunity: reconcile discrepancies, clarify relationships)
[ ] II-D-07: Theoretical Gap — can existing theories not explain new phenomena?
    (Opportunity: extend, modify, or develop new theoretical frameworks)
[ ] II-D-08: Population Gap — are certain groups underrepresented?
    (Opportunity: broaden scope to include diverse populations)
[ ] II-D-09: Does my review build toward a clear gap statement?
    (The gap should be the logical conclusion of the synthesis)
[ ] II-D-10: Have I explained WHY this gap matters?
    (It's not enough to say "X hasn't been studied" — say why it should be)
[ ] II-D-11: Have I formulated an original perspective from my unique combination of sources?
[ ] II-D-12: Is my gap clearly distinguishable from what others have already done?
[ ] II-D-13: Have I identified a gap that is specific enough to be researchable?
[ ] II-D-14: Have I considered that "no literature" likely means "not viable," not "gap"?
[ ] II-D-15: Have I positioned my research as the natural response to this gap?
```

## Part III: Methodology

---

### III-A: Research Design

Based on: Quantitative, qualitative, mixed methods, IMRaD, when to choose each.

```
[ ] III-A-01: Did I start with my research question, not my preferred method?
    (Methods are tools to answer questions — choice flows from the question)
[ ] III-A-02: Have I determined whether my question is exploratory, descriptive, explanatory, or comparative?
[ ] III-A-03: Exploratory ("What is happening?") → consider qualitative methods
[ ] III-A-04: Descriptive ("How much? How many?") → consider quantitative methods
[ ] III-A-05: Explanatory ("Why? What is the relationship?") → consider quantitative
[ ] III-A-06: Comparative ("How do groups differ?") → consider quantitative
[ ] III-A-07: Have I considered mixed methods if one approach is insufficient?
    (When you need both breadth of trends and depth of context)
[ ] III-A-08: If mixed methods, have I chosen a design?
    (Sequential — one after another; Concurrent — simultaneously)
[ ] III-A-09: Have I matched my method to my purpose?
    (Test hypothesis → experimental; Understand experiences → qualitative)
[ ] III-A-10: Have I considered my audience and discipline's conventions?
    (Natural sciences → quantitative; Humanities → qualitative; Social sciences → mixed)
[ ] III-A-11: Have I chosen a method I can implement with confidence?
    (If lacking a skill, plan for training or collaboration)
[ ] III-A-12: Have I planned my data collection AND analysis together?
    (Think about analysis early to collect the right data)
[ ] III-A-13: Have I acknowledged that all methods have limitations?
    (Your job is to mitigate issues and recognize them honestly)
[ ] III-A-14: Have I leveraged my personal strengths?
    (Skilled in statistics → lean quantitative; Skilled in interviews → lean qualitative)
[ ] III-A-15: Have I documented my research design in detail in my methodology chapter?
```

---

### III-B: Qualitative Approaches

Based on: "Big Five" designs + other notable approaches.

```
[ ] III-B-01: Have I chosen which qualitative approach is appropriate?
[ ] III-B-02: Narrative Research — am I exploring individual lives through stories?
    (Best for: understanding a single person's meaning-making over time)
[ ] III-B-03: Have I considered how to collect narrative data?
    (In-depth interviews, personal documents, archival materials)
[ ] III-B-04: Phenomenology — am I understanding the lived experience of a phenomenon?
    (Best for: describing the essence of a shared human experience)
[ ] III-B-05: Have I considered which phenomenological approach?
    (Descriptive/transcendental vs. interpretive/hermeneutical)
[ ] III-B-06: Grounded Theory — am I developing a theory from the ground up?
    (Best for: studying a process with no adequate existing theory)
[ ] III-B-07: Am I prepared for constant comparative analysis until theoretical saturation?
[ ] III-B-08: Ethnography — am I describing cultural patterns of a group?
    (Best for: understanding a culture from an insider's perspective)
[ ] III-B-09: Am I prepared for extended immersion and participant observation?
[ ] III-B-10: Case Study — am I exploring a single bounded system in depth?
    (Best for: understanding a specific case in its real-life context)
[ ] III-B-11: Have I determined what "the case" is?
    (A person, group, organization, event, program — clearly bounded)
[ ] III-B-12: Have I considered the type of case study?
    (Intrinsic, instrumental, or collective?)
[ ] III-B-13: Have I considered other approaches?
    (Action research, autoethnography, content analysis, historical research)
[ ] III-B-14: Have I ensured my chosen approach aligns with my research question?
[ ] III-B-15: Have I considered my philosophical stance?
    (Different approaches carry different ontological/epistemological assumptions)
[ ] III-B-16: Have I documented my data collection procedures in detail?
    (Interviews, focus groups, observations, document analysis)
[ ] III-B-17: Have I planned how to analyze qualitative data?
    (Thematic analysis, content analysis, discourse analysis, etc.)
[ ] III-B-18: Have I considered trustworthiness criteria?
    (Credibility, transferability, dependability, confirmability)
[ ] III-B-19: Have I considered my role as the research instrument?
    (Reflexivity, positionality, researcher bias)
[ ] III-B-20: Have I consulted key methodological texts?
    (e.g., Creswell & Poth, 2018 for the "Big Five")
```

### III-C: Quantitative Analysis

Based on: Descriptive + inferential statistics, parametric assumptions, test selection.

```
[ ] III-C-01: Have I written out my research questions in operational terms?
    (Vague questions = wrong statistical method = wasted data)
[ ] III-C-02: Have I formulated null (H₀) and alternative (H₁) hypotheses?
[ ] III-C-03: Have I chosen my research design?
    (Experimental, correlational, or descriptive?)
[ ] III-C-04: Have I determined the level of measurement for each variable?
    (Nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio — determines which test is valid)
[ ] III-C-05: Have I written an analysis plan before data collection?
    (Best guess for the statistical method — doesn't need to be final)
[ ] III-C-06: Have I calculated sample size before collecting data?
    (Based on planned statistical tests, power, and effect size)
[ ] III-C-07: Have I planned my data entry procedure?
    (How will data be entered, coded, and stored?)
[ ] III-C-08: Have I cleaned my dataset?
    (Missing values, out-of-range values, duplications, anomalies)
[ ] III-C-09: Have I handled missing data appropriately?
    (Removal, imputation, or statistical management — documented transparently)
[ ] III-C-10: Have I run descriptive statistics first?
    (Frequencies, central tendency, dispersion — understand data before testing)
[ ] III-C-11: Have I visualized my data?
    (Histograms, box plots, bar charts — check distributions and outliers)
[ ] III-C-12: Have I verified parametric assumptions?
    (Normality, homogeneity of variance, independence, linearity)
[ ] III-C-13: If assumptions are violated, have I considered non-parametric alternatives?
[ ] III-C-14: Have I chosen the correct inferential test?
    (t-tests, ANOVA, correlation, regression, chi-square — based on variables)
[ ] III-C-15: Have I chosen inferential tests based on my data type and assumptions?
[ ] III-C-16: Have I avoided p-hacking / fishing expeditions?
    (Don't search for significant results and report only those)
[ ] III-C-17: Have I avoided overcomplicating methods?
    (Simpler models that fit the data are preferred)
[ ] III-C-18: Have I avoided incorrect tests for small sample sizes?
[ ] III-C-19: Have I avoided poor reporting of missing data?
    (Must be transparent and justified)
[ ] III-C-20: Have I chosen appropriate software?
    (SPSS, R, Stata, Excel — based on competence and requirements)
[ ] III-C-21: Have I interpreted my findings in my discussion?
    (What do the numbers mean in context of your research question?)
[ ] III-C-22: Have I reported ALL results — significant and non-significant?
[ ] III-C-23: Have I used tables and figures effectively?
    (Clear, self-explanatory, with proper legends and labels)
[ ] III-C-24: Have I avoided data dumping?
    (Don't repeat everything in visuals — highlight key findings)
[ ] III-C-25: Have I ended the results section with a summary linking to discussion?
```

### III-D: Sampling

Based on: Probability and non-probability methods, sample size, bias.

```
[ ] III-D-01: Have I clearly defined my target population?
    (The entire group you intend to study and draw conclusions about)
[ ] III-D-02: Have I defined my sampling frame?
    (The list of all units with a chance of being selected)
[ ] III-D-03: Have I chosen probability or non-probability sampling based on my goal?
    (Generalize → probability; Explore/hard-to-reach → non-probability)
[ ] III-D-04: Simple Random — is my population small and do I have a complete list?
    (Every member has equal chance; minimal bias)
[ ] III-D-05: Systematic — do I need an efficient method for a large population?
    (Select every nth person — check for hidden patterns in the list)
[ ] III-D-06: Stratified — do I need to ensure specific subgroups are represented?
    (Divide into strata, then randomly sample from each)
[ ] III-D-07: Cluster — is my population large and geographically dispersed?
    (Randomly select clusters, include all members in chosen clusters)
[ ] III-D-08: Multistage — is this an extension of cluster sampling needed?
    (Additional random sampling within selected clusters)
[ ] III-D-09: Convenience — is this a pilot study with extreme time/resource limits?
    (Acknowledge high risk of bias)
[ ] III-D-10: Purposive — do I need specialized knowledge from a particular group?
    (Select based on the specific purpose of the study)
[ ] III-D-11: Snowball — am I studying a hard-to-reach or hidden population?
    (Start with a few, ask for referrals)
[ ] III-D-12: Quota — do I need to ensure specific variables are represented?
    (Set quotas, fill with convenience sampling within each)
[ ] III-D-13: Have I determined sample size based on population, effect size, and power?
[ ] III-D-14: Have I accounted for Type I and Type II errors in my sample size?
[ ] III-D-15: Have I used a sample size calculator or software?
[ ] III-D-16: Have I considered and planned to mitigate sampling bias?
    (Selection bias, non-response bias, coverage bias)
[ ] III-D-17: Have I maximized response rates to reduce non-response bias?
[ ] III-D-18: Have I ensured my sampling frame adequately covers the population?
    (Coverage bias — frame doesn't match population)
[ ] III-D-19: Have I documented my sampling procedure in detail?
[ ] III-D-20: Have I acknowledged the limitations of my sampling method?
    (Especially if using non-probability sampling)
```

```

[ ] III-E-02: Are my criteria clear and easy to apply?
[ ] III-E-03: Are my criteria directly informed by my research question?
[ ] III-E-04: Have I used a framework to structure my criteria?

[ ] III-E-05: Population — have I specified age, gender, condition, demographics?
[ ] III-E-06: Intervention/Exposure — have I specified the treatment or condition?
[ ] III-E-07: Comparator — have I specified comparison groups?
[ ] III-E-08: Outcomes — have I specified measured results of interest?
[ ] III-E-09: Study Design — have I specified acceptable methodological approaches?
[ ] III-E-10: Geography/Setting — have I specified countries or care settings?
[ ] III-E-11: Language — have I specified languages and justified restrictions?

[ ] III-E-16: Have I avoided arbitrary restrictions that introduce bias?
[ ] III-E-17: Have I avoided criteria that are too broad?

[ ] III-E-18: Have I avoided criteria that are too narrow?

[ ] III-E-19: Have I documented my criteria in my protocol and methods section?
[ ] III-E-20: Will I track and report reasons for all excluded full-text articles?
    (Transparency and reproducibility)
[ ] III-E-21: Have I considered using a PRISMA flow diagram to report screening?
```

## Part IV: Writing & Structure

---

### IV-A: Abstract

Based on: 5-part structure (Background, Objective, Methods, Results, Conclusion).

```
[ ] IV-A-01: Did I write the abstract LAST?
    (After completing the full paper — it summarizes a concluded study)
[ ] IV-A-02: Does my abstract include all five core components?
    (Background, Objective, Methods, Results, Conclusion)
[ ] IV-A-03: Background — does it establish context and why the problem matters?
    (One or two sentences setting the scene)
[ ] IV-A-04: Objective — does it state the research question or purpose?
    (Clear, specific, not vague)
[ ] IV-A-05: Methods — does it briefly describe design, sample, and procedures?
    (Enough for readers to gauge credibility, not full replication detail)
[ ] IV-A-06: Results — does it report key findings with specific data?
    (This is the most critical part — include effect sizes, means, p-values)
[ ] IV-A-07: Conclusion — does it explain significance and implications?
    (The "So What?" — why these findings matter)
[ ] IV-A-08: Is my abstract within the word limit?
    (Typically 150-300 words — check target venue)
[ ] IV-A-09: Is it self-contained and understandable without reading the full paper?
[ ] IV-A-10: Is it written in the past tense?
    (Describing completed research — "The study found...")
[ ] IV-A-11: Does it include key terms for discoverability?
    (Terms researchers would search for in databases)
[ ] IV-A-12: Have I avoided jargon and acronyms?
    (Define on first use if unavoidable)
[ ] IV-A-13: Have I avoided citations in the abstract?
    (Generally not needed — save for the main text)
[ ] IV-A-14: Have I followed the target venue's specific guidelines?
    (Structured vs. unstructured, word count, format)
[ ] IV-A-15: Is the abstract a compelling "trailer" for the full paper?
    (Does it make readers want to read more?)
[ ] IV-A-16: Have I avoided being too vague?
    ("We found a difference" → "We found a 23% increase, p < 0.01")
[ ] IV-A-17: Have I avoided including information not in the paper?
```

### IV-B: Introduction

Based on: Funnel model, 5 essential parts, thesis roadmap.

```
[ ] IV-B-01: Does my introduction follow the funnel structure?
    (Broad → specific → narrow focus of your research)
[ ] IV-B-02: PART 1 — Contextualization and Background: have I set the scene?
    (Broad field, relevance, "hook" — statistic, event, question)
[ ] IV-B-03: Have I avoided starting with a dictionary definition?
    (Overly common mistake — shows lack of depth)
[ ] IV-B-04: Have I avoided overly broad claims not specific to my study?
[ ] IV-B-05: PART 2 — Research Problem and Gap: have I clearly identified the problem?
    ("What is not known?" — the gap your study fills)
[ ] IV-B-06: Is the problem statement a question that can't be answered with yes/no?
[ ] IV-B-07: PART 3 — Research Objectives and Questions: are they clearly stated?
    (The specific "bottleneck" of your funnel)
[ ] IV-B-08: PART 4 — Significance and Rationale: have I answered "So What?"?
    (Impact on theory, practice, or policy)
[ ] IV-B-09: PART 5 — Thesis Outline and Scope: does it provide a roadmap?
    (Brief chapter-by-chapter preview and delimitations)
[ ] IV-B-10: Is my introduction proportionate to the rest of the thesis?
    (~10% of total word count for thesis; shorter for journal articles)
[ ] IV-B-11: Did I write (or at least revise) the introduction last?
    (You need a complete understanding of your research first)
[ ] IV-B-12: Have I synthesized findings rather than summarizing one by one?
    (Introduction is NOT a mini-literature review — synthesize)
[ ] IV-B-13: Have I used a citation manager to organize sources as I go?
[ ] IV-B-14: Is the writing crisp, active, and free of unnecessary jargon?
[ ] IV-B-15: Does the introduction complement the conclusion?
    (They are natural partners — written to bookend the thesis)
[ ] IV-B-16: Have I included the thesis structure roadmap in the last paragraph?
```

### IV-C: Methodology Chapter

Based on: 10 components, replication detail, justification.

```
[ ] IV-C-01: Have I distinguished between methodology and methods?
    (Methodology = overarching framework; Methods = specific tools)
[ ] IV-C-02: Does the chapter begin by restating the research problem and questions?
[ ] IV-C-03: Have I included my research design and approach?
    (Qualitative/quantitative/mixed, design strategy)
[ ] IV-C-04: Have I described the study context?
    (Time, place, setting)
[ ] IV-C-05: Have I described the population and sampling strategy?
    (Who/what, sample characteristics, sample size, inclusion/exclusion)
[ ] IV-C-06: Have I described data collection methods in detail?
    (Instruments, materials, step-by-step procedures)
[ ] IV-C-07: Have I described data analysis procedures?
    (How raw data was processed, analytical techniques)
[ ] IV-C-08: Have I addressed ethical considerations?
    (Consent, confidentiality, data protection, ethics approval)
[ ] IV-C-09: Have I acknowledged limitations honestly?
    (Sample, time, access, biases — show you mitigated them)
[ ] IV-C-10: Have I justified EVERY methodological choice?
    (Not just "I used X" but "I used X because Y, and considered Z but rejected it because...")
[ ] IV-C-11: Have I provided enough detail for replication?
    (Someone else should be able to repeat your study)
[ ] IV-C-12: Have I written this chapter in the past tense?
    (Methods are completed actions)
[ ] IV-C-13: Have I used subheadings for organization?
    (Clear, logical, easy to navigate)
[ ] IV-C-14: Have I cited literature to support my methodological choices?
[ ] IV-C-15: Have I included a summary at the end signposting the next chapter?
[ ] IV-C-16: Did I write this chapter early in the research process?
    (While procedures were fresh — keep a research diary)
```

### IV-D: Results → Discussion → Conclusion → Recommendations

Based on: Upright pyramid, common pitfalls, each section's distinct purpose.

#### Results
```
[ ] IV-D-01: Have I presented findings objectively without interpretation?
    (Results = what you found; Discussion = what it means)
[ ] IV-D-02: Have I re-stated research questions or hypotheses at the start?
[ ] IV-D-03: Are findings presented in a logical order?
    (Typically by research question or objective)
[ ] IV-D-04: Have I used visuals (tables, figures, graphs) effectively?
    (Self-explanatory, clear titles, legends, axis labels)
[ ] IV-D-05: Have I included negative/non-significant results?
    (They contribute to the full picture)
[ ] IV-D-06: Have I avoided data dumping?
    (Highlight key findings — don't repeat everything from visuals)
[ ] IV-D-07: Does the results section end with a summary linking to discussion?
[ ] IV-D-08: Have I included descriptive statistics before inferential tests?
[ ] IV-D-09: Have I avoided "cherry-picking" only supportive results?
```

#### Discussion
```
[ ] IV-D-10: Does the discussion interpret findings — NOT restate results?
    (Discussion = intellectual core, not repetition)
[ ] IV-D-11: Have I started by stating key findings?
    (Concise summary to set up interpretation)
[ ] IV-D-12: Have I compared my findings with prior research?
    (Agreement, disagreement, and WHY)
[ ] IV-D-13: Have I addressed unexpected results honestly?
[ ] IV-D-14: Have I explained what my findings mean for the field?
    (Balanced — avoid boosterism AND over-caution)
[ ] IV-D-15: Have I acknowledged limitations?
    (No study is flawless — honesty improves credibility)
[ ] IV-D-16: Have I used the "upright pyramid" structure?
    (Specific findings → broader implications — opposite of Introduction)
[ ] IV-D-17: Have I answered the research questions posed in the Introduction?
[ ] IV-D-18: Have I included original arguments, not just recycled ideas?
[ ] IV-D-19: Have I proposed specific future research directions?
```

#### Conclusion
```
[ ] IV-D-20: Does the conclusion synthesize — not just repeat?
    (Concise closure emphasizing significance)
[ ] IV-D-21: Have I provided a succinct answer to my research question?
[ ] IV-D-22: Have I explicitly stated my novel contribution?
    ("My original contribution to knowledge is...")
[ ] IV-D-23: Have I avoided introducing new ideas or evidence?
[ ] IV-D-24: Have I kept the conclusion simple and short?
    (Most journals recommend concise conclusions)
[ ] IV-D-25: Have I avoided starting with "In conclusion"?
    (Cliché — just start with the substance)
[ ] IV-D-26: Does my conclusion align with my introduction?
    (They are natural bookends)
[ ] IV-D-27: Have I used assertive, confident language?
    ("The findings show..." rather than "It is believed that...")
```

#### Recommendations
```
[ ] IV-D-28: Are my recommendations specific and actionable?
    (WHAT is to be done, WHO is to do it, HOW)
[ ] IV-D-29: Are my recommendations based directly on my findings?
    (Every suggestion must stem from your work)
[ ] IV-D-30: Have I tied each recommendation to evidence?
[ ] IV-D-31: Have I prioritized the most important recommendations?
[ ] IV-D-32: Have I avoided vague suggestions like "more research is needed"?
    (Be specific about what kind and why)
[ ] IV-D-33: Have I offered recommendations for practice AND future research?
[ ] IV-D-34: Are my recommendations tailored to my audience?
```

### IV-F: Verb Tenses

Based on: Tense usage by section (APA 7th edition and other academic sources).

```
[ ] IV-F-01: Abstract — have I used past tense for what I did and found?
    ("The study was conducted...")
[ ] IV-F-02: Abstract — have I used present tense for conclusions and implications?
    ("The findings suggest...")
[ ] IV-F-03: Introduction — have I used present tense for established facts?
    ("C. heterophylla is a plant with ant domata...")
[ ] IV-F-04: Introduction — have I used past tense for previous studies?
    ("Smith (2019) found that...")
[ ] IV-F-05: Literature Review — past tense for specific studies by specific authors?
    ("Martin (2020) addressed...")
[ ] IV-F-06: Literature Review — present perfect for general body of research?
    ("Researchers have studied...")
[ ] IV-F-07: Literature Review — present tense for general truths?
    ("Research shows that...")
[ ] IV-F-08: Methodology — have I used past tense throughout?
    ("Participants took a survey... responses were collected...")
[ ] IV-F-09: Results — have I used past tense?
    ("Results showed... scores decreased...")
[ ] IV-F-10: Discussion — present tense for interpretation and implications?
    ("The results indicate... the findings mean that...")
[ ] IV-F-11: Discussion — past tense for summarizing results?
    ("Data was collected...")
[ ] IV-F-12: Conclusion — present tense for final statements?
    ("We conclude...")
[ ] IV-F-13: Conclusion — future tense for future research?
    ("Future research should explore...")
[ ] IV-F-14: Have I been consistent within each section?
    (Avoid switching tenses without reason)
[ ] IV-F-15: Have I avoided future tense in my methodology?
    (Writing "participants will be recruited" suggests the study isn't done)
[ ] IV-F-16: Have I converted my proposal from future to past tense?
    (Proposal: "We aim to investigate" → Manuscript: "We investigated")
[ ] IV-F-17: Have I used the "literary present" for discussing authors' arguments?
    ("Smith argues that..." — their ideas are still relevant)
[ ] IV-F-18: Have I checked discipline-specific conventions?
    (Some fields may differ from general guidelines)
```
